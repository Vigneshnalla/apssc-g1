# Preamble

The American Constitution was the first Constitution to begin with a Preamble. Many countries, including India, followed this practice.

The term **Preamble** refers to the introduction or preface of the Constitution. It contains the basic philosophy, ideals, objectives and essence of the Constitution.

### N.A. Palkhivala

N.A. Palkhivala, an eminent jurist and constitutional expert, described the Preamble as the **“identity card of the Constitution.”**

The Preamble to the Indian Constitution is based on the **‘Objectives Resolution’**, drafted and moved by Pandit Nehru, and adopted by the Constituent Assembly.

**Moved:** 13 December 1946
**Adopted:** 22 January 1947

The Preamble was amended by the **42nd Constitutional Amendment Act (1976)**, which added three new words—**Socialist, Secular and Integrity**.

> **“WE, THE PEOPLE OF INDIA, having solemnly resolved to constitute India into a SOVEREIGN SOCIALIST SECULAR DEMOCRATIC REPUBLIC and to secure to all its citizens:**
>
> **JUSTICE, social, economic and political;**
>
> **LIBERTY of thought, expression, belief, faith and worship;**
>
> **EQUALITY of status and of opportunity;**
>
> **and to promote among them all**
>
> **FRATERNITY assuring the dignity of the individual and the unity and integrity of the Nation;**
>
> **IN OUR CONSTITUENT ASSEMBLY this twenty-sixth day of November, 1949, do HEREBY ADOPT, ENACT AND GIVE TO OURSELVES THIS CONSTITUTION.”**

The Constituent Assembly formally adopted the Constitution on **26 November 1949**.

## Four Ingredients of the Preamble

**“The Preamble reveals four ingredients or components.”**

1. **Source of authority of the Constitution:** The Preamble states that the Constitution derives its authority from the people of India.

2. **Nature of the Indian State:** It declares India to be a sovereign, socialist, secular, democratic and republican polity.

3. **Objectives of the Constitution:** It specifies justice, liberty, equality and fraternity as the objectives.

4. **Date of adoption of the Constitution:** It stipulates **26 November 1949** as the date.

## Key Words

## 1. SOVEREIGN

The word **‘sovereign’** implies that India is neither a dependency nor a dominion of any other nation, but an independent state.

Sovereign means that India is an independent state with supreme authority over its own affairs.

Though in **1949**, India declared the continuation of her full membership of the Commonwealth of Nations and accepted the British Crown as the head of the Commonwealth, this extra-constitutional declaration does not affect India’s sovereignty in any manner.

This did not mean that Britain had authority over India. India remained a sovereign and independent country.

Further, India’s membership of the **United Nations Organisation (UNO) [1945]** also in no way constitutes a limitation on her sovereignty.

Being a sovereign state, India can either acquire a foreign territory or cede a part of its territory in favour of a foreign state.

**Note:** A dependency is a territory controlled by another country, and a dominion was a country that had self-government but still had a constitutional relationship with the British Crown. India is neither.

---

## 2. SOCIALIST

Even before the term **‘socialist’** was added by the **42nd Amendment in 1976**, the Constitution already contained socialist-oriented provisions, particularly in the **Directive Principles of State Policy (DPSP)**.

It was **implicit earlier**, but in **1976, it became explicit**.

India follows **democratic socialism**, rather than a communist form of socialism.

### Democratic Socialism Seeks To:

* reduce poverty and inequality;
* promote social and economic welfare;
* provide equal opportunities;
* maintain democracy; and
* allow both public and private sectors to exist.

Democratic socialism holds faith in a **‘mixed economy’**, where both public and private sectors coexist side-by-side.

The Supreme Court has described democratic socialism as aiming to end poverty, ignorance, disease and inequality of opportunity. Indian socialism is a blend of **Marxism and Gandhism**, leaning heavily towards **Gandhian socialism**.

### 1991 Economic Reforms

The New Economic Policy of 1991 introduced:

* **Liberalisation**
* **Privatisation**
* **Globalisation**

These reforms increased the role of the private sector and reduced several government controls over the economy.

Therefore, the economic meaning of Indian socialism has evolved over time.

### Prelims Line

**Socialist = social/economic welfare + reduction of inequality + equal opportunities + coexistence of public and private sectors.**

---

## 3. SECULAR

The word **“Secular”** was added to the Preamble by the **42nd Constitutional Amendment Act, 1976**.

However, the Constitution had already incorporated secular principles before 1976.

### Meaning of Secularism in India

India is a **secular state**, meaning it has **no official state religion** and gives **equal respect and protection to all religions**.

The Constitution provides for **freedom of religion** and **equal treatment of religions**.

**Articles 25 to 28** deal with **freedom of religion**.

### Three Types of States Based on Religion

### 1. Atheistic State

A state that rejects or opposes religion.

### 2. Theocratic State

A state that gives a particular religion an official or dominant position.

### 3. Secular State

A state that does not establish a particular religion as the state religion and provides for **religious freedom and equal treatment**.

### H.R. Gokhale

His explanation emphasizes that:

* every person should have freedom of faith and worship;
* the State should not favour one religion; and
* all religions should be treated equally.

### P.B. Gajendragadkar

The Indian State does not owe special loyalty or preference to one particular religion.

However, secularism does not mean being **anti-religious** or ignoring religion.

### Prelims Line

**Indian secularism = religious freedom + no state religion + equal treatment/protection of religions.**

---

## 4. DEMOCRATIC

A **democratic polity** [system of government], as stipulated in the Preamble, means that **the people are the ultimate source of political power**.

The term **“democracy”** is derived from two Greek words, namely **Demos** and **Kratia**, meaning **“people”** and **“rule”**, respectively.

### Democracy Can Be Broadly Divided Into:

### 1. Direct Democracy

In **direct democracy**, people **directly participate in making important decisions**, instead of leaving all decisions to elected representatives, as in the case of **Switzerland**.

There are four important devices of direct democracy:

**Referendum** = People directly vote on a proposed law or important proposal.

**Initiative** = People themselves can propose a bill and ask the legislature to consider making it a law.

**Recall** = People can remove an elected representative before the representative's term ends, where such a system exists.

**Plebiscite** = A method of asking the people directly for their opinion on an important public issue. It is generally used in connection with **territorial disputes**.

### 2. Indirect Democracy

In **indirect democracy**, people **elect representatives**, and those representatives run the government and make laws on behalf of the people.

It has two major forms:

* **Parliamentary democracy**
* **Presidential democracy**

The Indian Constitution provides for **representative parliamentary democracy**, under which the **executive is responsible to the legislature for all its policies and actions**.

**Executive** = The part of government that runs the administration and implements laws.

**Legislature** = The body that makes laws.

### Features of India's Democratic System

These are some features that show the democratic nature of India's system:

**Universal adult franchise** → Adult citizens have the right to vote, subject to legal qualifications.

**Periodic elections** → Elections take place at regular intervals.

**Rule of law** → Everyone is governed by law.

**Independence of judiciary** → Courts function independently.

### Democratic Means More Than Elections

The word **“democratic”** in the Preamble means more than just **elections and voting**.

It also means **political equality, social equality, and fair economic opportunities** for people.

Dr. B.R. Ambedkar said this in his Constituent Assembly speech on **November 25, 1949**:

> “Political democracy cannot last unless there lies at the base of it social democracy.”

**Social democracy** means a society that respects **freedom, equality, and fraternity**.

**Liberty** = Freedom

**Equality** = Equal status

**Fraternity** = Feeling of brotherhood and togetherness

**Trinity** = A group of three closely connected things.

Liberty, equality, and fraternity form a **trinity**, meaning that these three principles are closely connected. If one is separated from the others, the basic purpose of democracy is weakened.

### Supreme Court and Social Democracy

The Supreme Court has emphasized the constitutional goal of creating an **egalitarian social order** based on **social, economic, and political justice**.

In **Samatha v. State of Andhra Pradesh (1997)**, the Supreme Court discussed the constitutional goal of establishing an egalitarian social order and securing socio-economic justice, particularly for weaker sections of society.

## 5. REPUBLIC

A democratic system can have either a **monarchy** or a **republic**, depending on how the head of the state gets the position.

In a monarchy, the head of the state is usually a **king or queen**. The position is generally passed from one member of the royal family to another.

In a republic, on the other hand, the head of the state is **elected directly or indirectly for a fixed period**.

Therefore, the term **‘republic’** in our Preamble indicates that its head of state is the **President**, who is elected rather than inheriting the position.

A republic also means two more things: one, **vesting [giving something to someone] of political sovereignty in the people** and not in a single individual like a king.

A republic does not **accept a special privileged class** that has an automatic right to government positions.

Public offices are open to citizens **without discrimination**, subject to the required qualifications.

* **Privileged class** = a group given special advantages or rights.
* **Discrimination** = unfair treatment based on certain characteristics.

---

## 6. Justice

The term **‘justice’** in the Preamble embraces [includes] three distinct forms—**social, economic and political**.

The Constitution supports these through various provisions of **Fundamental Rights** and **Directive Principles of State Policy**.

### Social Justice

Social justice denotes the **equal treatment of all citizens without any social distinction** based on caste, colour, race, religion, sex and so on.

Social justice means that one particular social group should not receive unfair special privileges. It also means taking steps to improve the conditions of backward classes such as **SCs, STs, OBCs and women**.

### Economic Justice

Economic justice denotes the **non-discrimination between people on the basis of economic factors**.

### Political Justice

Political justice implies that all citizens should have **equal political rights, equal access to all political offices and equal voice in the government**.

The ideal of justice—**social, economic and political**—has been taken from the **Russian Revolution (1917)**.

---

## 7. Liberty

The term **‘liberty’** means the **absence of restraints on the activities of individuals**, and at the same time, providing opportunities for the development of individual personalities.

The Preamble promises to all citizens of India **liberty of thought, expression, belief, faith and worship**.

These freedoms are protected through **Fundamental Rights**, which can be enforced through courts when they are violated.

> “Liberty as elaborated in the Preamble is very essential for the successful functioning of the Indian democratic system.”

Liberty does not mean that a person can do absolutely anything they want.

Freedom must be exercised within the limits allowed by the **Constitution and law**.

The freedom given by the Preamble and Fundamental Rights is **not unlimited**. It is subject to certain constitutional restrictions.

---

## 8. Equality

The term **‘equality’** means the **absence of special privileges to any section of society** and the provision of **adequate opportunities for all individuals without any discrimination**.

The Preamble secures to all citizens of India **equality of status and opportunity**.

### The Idea of Equality Has Three Dimensions:

1. **Civic equality**
2. **Political equality**
3. **Economic equality**

**Civic** = Related to the rights and position of citizens in society.

### Civic Equality

The following provisions on **Fundamental Rights** ensure civic equality:

#### 1. Equality Before Law — Article 14

Everyone is subject to the same law, and the law must provide equal legal protection.

#### 2. Prohibition of Discrimination — Article 15

Discrimination is prohibited on grounds of religion, race, caste, sex or place of birth.

#### 3. Equality of Opportunity in Matters of Public Employment — Article 16

#### 4. Abolition of Untouchability — Article 17

#### 5. Abolition of Titles — Article 18

---

### Political Equality

There are two provisions in the Constitution that seek to achieve political equality.

First, a person cannot be excluded from the electoral roll simply because of their religion, race, caste or sex.

**Electoral roll** = Official list of people who are registered to vote.

**Ineligible** = Not allowed or qualified.

Second, elections to the **Lok Sabha and the State Assemblies** are to be conducted on the basis of **adult suffrage (Article 326)**.

---

### Economic Equality

The **Directive Principles of State Policy (Article 39)** secure to men and women an **equal right to an adequate means of livelihood** and **equal pay for equal work**.

---

## 9. Fraternity

**“Fraternity means a sense of brotherhood.”**

The Constitution promotes this feeling of fraternity through the system of **single citizenship**.

Also, the **Fundamental Duties (Article 51-A)** say that it shall be the duty of every citizen of India to promote harmony and the spirit of common brotherhood amongst all the people of India, transcending religious, linguistic, regional or sectional diversities.

The Preamble declares that fraternity has to assure two things—the **dignity of the individual** and the **unity and integrity of the nation**.

**Dignity** = The value and respect that every person deserves.

**Integrity** = Wholeness and unity of the nation.

---

## Dignity of the Individual and Unity and Integrity of the Nation

### Dignity of the Individual

**K.M. Munshi:** The Constitution is not concerned only with **material improvement** or maintaining democracy. It also recognizes the **inherent worth of every individual** and the need to respect every person.

* **Inherent worth** → The natural value of every person simply because they are a human being.
* **Dignity of individual** → Treating every person with respect and recognizing their value.

The **Fundamental Rights (FRs)** and **Directive Principles of State Policy (DPSP)** help ensure the **dignity of the individual**.

The **Fundamental Duties** also make it the duty of every citizen to **uphold and protect the sovereignty, unity and integrity of India**.

### Unity and Integrity of the Nation

The phrase **“unity and integrity of the nation”** has two dimensions:

* **Psychological unity** → People feel that they belong to one nation.
* **Territorial unity** → The territory of India remains united.
* **National integration** → Bringing different people, communities and regions together as one nation.

**Article 1** describes India as a **“Union of States”**, indicating that states do not have a constitutional right to **secede** from India.

The Constitution seeks to overcome **hindrances to national integration**, such as:

* **Communalism** → Division or conflict based on religion.
* **Regionalism** → Excessive attachment to or preference for a particular region.
* **Casteism** → Discrimination or excessive preference based on caste.
* **Linguism** → Division or excessive preference based on language.
* **Secessionism** → The desire or movement to separate a territory from the country.
* **Hindrances** → Obstacles or difficulties.

**In short:** The Constitution aims to protect the **dignity of every individual** and maintain the **unity and integrity of India**.

## Preamble as Part of the Constitution

One of the controversies about the Preamble is whether it is a **part of the Constitution or not**.

### 1. Berubari Union Case — 1960

In the **Berubari Union case (1960)**, the Supreme Court held that the **Preamble was not a part of the Constitution**.

However, the Court held that the Preamble could be used as a **key to understand the intention of the Constitution-makers** and the purpose of the Constitution. If a constitutional provision is unclear, the Preamble can help in its interpretation.

⭐ **Key Point:**
**Berubari (1960) → Preamble was NOT a part of the Constitution.**

---

### 2. Kesavananda Bharati Case — 1973

In **Kesavananda Bharati v. State of Kerala (1973)**, the Supreme Court **changed its earlier view** and held that the **Preamble is a part of the Constitution**.

The Court also held that the Constitution should be **read and interpreted in the light of the ideals expressed in the Preamble**.

⭐ **Key Point:**
**Kesavananda Bharati (1973) → Preamble IS a part of the Constitution.**

---

## Amendability of the Preamble

Once the Supreme Court held that the **Preamble is a part of the Constitution**, another question arose:

> **Can Parliament amend the Preamble under Article 368?**

## Kesavananda Bharati Case — 1973

The Supreme Court held that the **Preamble can be amended under Article 368**.

However, Parliament's power to amend the Constitution is **not unlimited**. An amendment cannot **destroy or damage the Basic Structure of the Constitution**.

The **Basic Structure Doctrine** means that Parliament can amend the Constitution, but it cannot use its amending power to destroy its **basic foundation, essential features or identity**.

⭐ **Key Point:**
**Preamble can be amended → But its Basic Structure cannot be destroyed.**

## 3. 42nd Constitutional Amendment Act — 1976

The Preamble has been **amended only once so far**, by the **42nd Constitutional Amendment Act, 1976**.

It added three words to the Preamble:

* **Socialist**
* **Secular**
* **Integrity**

⭐ **Key Point:**
**42nd Amendment, 1976 → Only amendment to the Preamble so far.**

## Enactment of the Preamble

The Preamble was also adopted by the Constituent Assembly, but it was formally adopted **after the rest of the Constitution**.

This was done so that its wording would be consistent with the Constitution that had already been finalized.

Therefore, the present Supreme Court view is consistent with the intention of the Constitution-makers.

---

## Limitations of the Preamble

## 1. The Preamble is neither a source of power to the legislature nor a prohibition upon the powers of the legislature.

The Preamble does not give Parliament or State Legislatures the power to make laws, and it does not stop them from using the powers given to them by the Constitution.

## 2. It is non-justiciable, that is, its provisions are not enforceable in courts of law.

The Preamble is not directly enforceable in court. You cannot go to court and ask the court to enforce the Preamble itself.

**Preamble → gives direction/helps interpretation ✅**

**Preamble → itself gives you a right that you can directly enforce in court ❌**
