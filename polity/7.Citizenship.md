# Citizenship — Articles 5–11

**Part II of the Constitution — Articles 5 to 11**

> “Like any other modern state, India has two kinds of people: citizens and aliens.”

**Citizen** → a person who is legally recognised as a member of a country.

“Citizens are full members of the Indian State and owe allegiance to it.”

**Owe allegiance to India** = have a duty of loyalty towards India.

“They enjoy all civil and political rights.”

“Aliens are the citizens of some other state and hence, do not enjoy all the civil and political rights.”

They are of two categories—**friendly aliens** or **enemy aliens**.

**Friendly aliens** are the subjects of those countries that have cordial relations with India.

**Enemy aliens** are the subjects of a country that is at war with India.

“They enjoy lesser rights than the friendly aliens, e.g., they do not enjoy protection against arrest and detention.”

**Article 22** → It provides certain protections related to arrest and detention.

---

## Rights and Privileges of Citizens

“The Constitution confers [gives] the following rights and privileges on the citizens of India (and denies the same to aliens):”

1. **Right against discrimination** on grounds of religion, race, caste, sex, or place of birth (Article 15).

2. **Right to equality of opportunity** in matters of public employment (Article 16).

3. **Right to freedom** of speech and expression, assembly, association, movement, residence, and profession (Article 19).

4. **Cultural and educational rights** (Articles 29 and 30).

   For example, **Article 30** gives minorities the right to establish and administer educational institutions of their choice, subject to constitutional provisions.

5. **Right to vote** in elections to the Lok Sabha and State Legislative Assemblies.

6. **Right to contest** for membership of the Parliament and the State Legislature.

Certain important constitutional offices require the person to be an **Indian citizen**. These are:

* President of India
* Vice-President of India
* Judges of the Supreme Court and the High Courts
* Governors of States
* Attorney General of India [highest law officer of India]
* Advocate General of States [highest law officer of a State]

“Along with the above rights, the citizens also owe certain duties towards the Indian State, as, for example, paying taxes, respecting the national flag and national anthem, defending the country, and so on.”

“In India, both citizens by birth as well as naturalised citizens are eligible for the office of President, while in the USA, only a citizen by birth and not a naturalised citizen is eligible for the office of President.”

---

## Single Citizenship

Though India has a federal system with two levels of government—the Centre and the States—the Constitution provides for only one citizenship: **Indian citizenship**.

“The citizens in India owe allegiance only to the Union. There is no separate State citizenship.”

Other federal countries, such as the **USA and Australia**, have adopted the system of **double citizenship**, where a person can be a citizen of the country as well as of a particular State.

In countries with dual citizenship, citizens owe allegiance to both the country and their State. This may create the possibility of discrimination by a State in favour of its own citizens. India avoids this problem by providing for single citizenship, so there is no separate State citizenship.

---

## Equality of Citizenship in India

“In India, all citizens, irrespective of the State in which they are born or reside, enjoy the same political and civil rights of citizenship all over the country, and no discrimination is made between them.”

Citizens should not be discriminated against simply because they belong to a different State.

However, there are some exceptions.

---

## Exception 1 — Residence and Public Employment

The **Parliament, under Article 16, can require a person to be a resident of a particular State or Union Territory to be eligible for certain government jobs or appointments in that State, Union Territory, or its local authorities.**

Accordingly, Parliament enacted the **Public Employment (Requirement as to Residence) Act, 1957**, which authorised the Government of India to **specify residence as a qualification** for appointment to certain **non-Gazetted posts** in Andhra Pradesh, Himachal Pradesh, Manipur, and Tripura.

Although the Act expired in **1974**, a special provision regarding residence requirements continued to exist for **Andhra Pradesh and Telangana**.

---

## Exception 2 — Article 15 and Residence

“The Constitution (under Article 15) prohibits discrimination against any citizen on grounds of religion, race, caste, sex, or place of birth and not on the ground of residence.”

“This means that the State can provide special benefits or give preference to its residents in matters that are outside of the constitutional rights given by the Constitution to Indian citizens.”

---

“The freedom of movement and residence (under Article 19) is subject to the protection of the interests of any Scheduled Tribe.”

Article 19 gives citizens freedom of movement and residence.

But these freedoms can be restricted in certain areas to protect Scheduled Tribes.

“In other words, the right of outsiders to enter, reside, and settle in tribal areas is restricted.”

Of course, this is done to protect the distinctive culture, language, customs, and manners of Scheduled Tribes and to safeguard their traditional occupation and property against exploitation [unfairly using someone].

---

Until **2019**, the legislature of the **previous State of Jammu and Kashmir** was empowered to [given authority to]:

1. **Define who would be considered a permanent resident** of the State.

2. **Provide special rights and privileges** to such permanent residents regarding:

   * Employment under the State Government.
   * Acquisition of immovable property in the State.
   * Settlement in the State.
   * Scholarships and other State aid.

These special provisions were based on **Article 35-A of the Constitution**.

**Article 35-A** was introduced through the **Constitution (Application to Jammu and Kashmir) Order, 1954**, issued by the President under **Article 370**.

**Article 370** provided a special constitutional arrangement for the erstwhile State of Jammu and Kashmir.

In **2019**, the special constitutional arrangement was changed through the **Constitution (Application to Jammu and Kashmir) Order, 2019**, which superseded the earlier 1954 Order.

---

## SINGLE CITIZENSHIP

India follows **single citizenship**, like Canada. This means every person is simply an **Indian citizen**, not separately a citizen of a particular state.

The purpose of single citizenship is to promote **fraternity, unity and an integrated Indian nation**, rather than people being divided on the basis of state, language, caste, etc.

Even though India has single citizenship and uniform rights, India has still experienced social conflicts such as:

* **Communal riots** → conflicts between religious communities
* **Class conflicts** → conflicts between different economic/social classes
* **Caste wars** → conflicts based on caste
* **Linguistic clashes** → conflicts based on language
* **Ethnic disputes** → conflicts between different ethnic groups

---

## CONSTITUTIONAL PROVISIONS / SAFEGUARDS

The Constitution deals with **citizenship in Articles 5 to 11 under Part II**.

But these Articles do not provide **detailed and permanent rules** about citizenship.

The Constitution mainly identifies the persons who became citizens of India at its **commencement, i.e., on January 26, 1950**.

It does not give detailed rules about how a person can **acquire citizenship or lose citizenship after January 26, 1950**.

It **empowers Parliament to enact laws** to provide for such matters and any other matter relating to citizenship.

#### Important:

**Article 11 → Parliament can make laws regarding citizenship.**

**Citizenship Act → 1955**

Accordingly, the Parliament enacted the **Citizenship Act, 1955**, which has been **amended from time to time**.

→ In simple words, the Constitution gives the basic framework, while **Parliament makes detailed laws regarding citizenship**.

---

## ARTICLES 5–11 — CITIZENSHIP AT THE COMMENCEMENT OF THE CONSTITUTION

According to the Constitution, the following four categories of persons became the citizens of India at its commencement, i.e., on **January 26, 1950**:

### 1. Persons Domiciled in India — Article 5

A person could become an Indian citizen if the person had **domicile (permanent home) in India** and fulfilled any one of these three conditions:

* The person was born in India, **OR**
* Either parent was born in India, **OR**
* The person had been ordinarily living in India for **five years immediately before January 26, 1950**.

---

### 2. Persons Who Migrated from Pakistan to India — Article 6

A person who migrated to India from Pakistan became an Indian citizen if he/she, either of his/her parents, or any of his/her grandparents was born in **undivided India** and also fulfilled one of the following two conditions:

#### If he/she migrated to India before 19 July 1948:

He/she had to be **ordinarily residing in India from the date of migration**.

#### If he/she migrated to India on or after 19 July 1948:

He/she had to be **registered as an Indian citizen**.

However, for such registration, the person had to have been **residing in India for six months immediately before the date of applying for registration**.

---

### 3. Persons Who Migrated to Pakistan but Later Returned to India — Article 7

A person who migrated to Pakistan from India **after March 1, 1947**, but later returned to India **for resettlement**, could become an Indian citizen.

Such a person had to return to India under the required **permit for resettlement or permanent return** and fulfil the conditions for registration as an Indian citizen.

---

### 4. Persons of Indian Origin Residing Outside India — Article 8

A person who, or whose parent or grandparent, was born in **undivided India** and who was ordinarily residing outside India could become an Indian citizen if they were **registered as an Indian citizen by an Indian diplomatic or consular representative** in the country where they resided.

This registration could have been done either **before or after the commencement of the Constitution**.

---

### Article 9 — Voluntary Acquisition of Foreign Citizenship

If an Indian citizen **voluntarily acquires the citizenship of another country**, he/she cannot continue to be an Indian citizen under the Constitution.

---

### Article 10 — Continuance of Citizenship

Every person who is a citizen of India, or is considered to be a citizen under the earlier provisions of Part II, shall **continue to remain an Indian citizen**, subject to any law made by Parliament.

---

### Article 11 — Parliament's Power to Make Laws on Citizenship

The Constitution gives **Parliament the power to make provisions regarding the acquisition and termination of citizenship and all other matters relating to citizenship**.

→ Parliament can make detailed laws on citizenship.

---

## ACQUISITION OF CITIZENSHIP

The **Citizenship Act, 1955** prescribes five ways of acquiring citizenship:

1. **Birth**
2. **Descent**
3. **Registration**
4. **Naturalisation**
5. **Incorporation of Territory**

#### Memory: **B-D-R-N-I**

---

## 1. BY BIRTH

A person born in India on or after **January 26, 1950 but before July 1, 1987** is a citizen of India by birth irrespective of the nationality of his/her parents.

It did not matter whether the parents were **Indian citizens or foreigners**.

#### 26 January 1950 → before 1 July 1987

**Parents' nationality did not matter.**

---

A person born in India on or after **July 1, 1987 but before December 3, 2004** is considered as a citizen of India only if **either of his/her parents is a citizen of India at the time of his/her birth**.

#### 1 July 1987 → before 3 December 2004

**At least one parent must have been an Indian citizen when the child was born.**

---

Further, those born in India on or after **December 3, 2004** are considered citizens of India only if:

* **Both of their parents are citizens of India**, OR
* **One of their parents is a citizen of India and the other is not an illegal migrant** at the time of their birth.

#### From 3 December 2004

**Both parents Indian**

OR

**One parent Indian + other parent not an illegal migrant**

---

The children of **foreign diplomats posted in India** and **enemy aliens** cannot acquire Indian citizenship by birth.

---

## 2. BY DESCENT

Here, the person is **born outside India**, but gets Indian citizenship through their **Indian parent(s)**.

A person born outside India on or after **January 26, 1950 but before December 10, 1992** is a citizen of India by descent if his/her **father was a citizen of India at the time of his/her birth**.

#### Before 10 December 1992

**Father must be an Indian citizen.**

---

A person born outside India on or after **December 10, 1992** is considered as a citizen of India if **either of his/her parents is a citizen of India at the time of his/her birth**.

#### From 10 December 1992

**Either parent can be an Indian citizen.**

---

#### From 3 December 2004

There is an additional requirement.

A person born outside India must have their **birth registered at an Indian consulate within one year of birth**.

If the one-year period has expired, registration can be done **with the permission of the Central Government**.

When parents apply to register the birth of their minor child at an Indian consulate, they must give a **written undertaking** saying that the child does not hold a passport of another country.

---

A minor who becomes an Indian citizen by descent and is also a citizen of another country will lose Indian citizenship if, after attaining the age of **18**, he/she does not renounce the citizenship or nationality of the other country within **six months**.

---

## 3. BY REGISTRATION

The Central Government can register a person as an Indian citizen if that person belongs to one of the **specified categories**.

But the person **must not be an illegal migrant**.

#### (a) Person of Indian origin residing in India

A person of Indian origin who is ordinarily resident in India for **seven years** before making an application for registration.

→ A person of Indian origin living in India for seven years can apply for citizenship by registration.

#### (b) Person of Indian origin residing outside undivided India

A person of Indian origin who is ordinarily resident in **any country or place outside undivided India**.

→ A person of Indian origin living outside undivided India can apply for citizenship by registration.

#### (c) Person married to an Indian citizen

A person who is **married to a citizen of India** and is ordinarily resident in India for **seven years** before making an application for registration.

→ Marriage to an Indian citizen does not automatically give citizenship; the required residence condition must also be fulfilled.

#### (d) Minor children of Indian citizens

**Minor children of persons who are citizens of India.**

→ Minor children of Indian citizens can acquire citizenship by registration.

#### (e) Person whose parents are registered citizens

A person of full age and capacity whose **parents are registered as citizens of India**.

→ An adult whose parents have been registered as Indian citizens can apply for citizenship by registration.

#### (f) Earlier citizen of independent India

A person of full age and capacity who, or either of whose parents, was earlier a **citizen of independent India**, and who is ordinarily resident in India for **12 months immediately before making the application**.

→ A person with an earlier Indian citizenship connection can apply after living in India for 12 months.

#### (g) OCI cardholder

A person of full age and capacity who:

* has been registered as an **Overseas Citizen of India (OCI) cardholder for five years**, and
* has been **ordinarily resident in India for 12 months** before making the application.

→ An OCI cardholder can apply for Indian citizenship after fulfilling these requirements.

#### Person of Indian Origin

A person is considered to be of **Indian origin** if he/she, or either of his/her parents, was born in **undivided India** or in such other territory which became part of India after **August 15, 1947**.

#### Oath of Allegiance

A person applying for citizenship by registration has to take an **oath of allegiance to the Constitution of India**.

→ The person formally promises loyalty to the Constitution.

---

## 4. BY NATURALISATION

The Central Government may grant a **certificate of naturalisation** to any person, not being an illegal migrant, if he/she possesses the required qualifications.

#### (a) Reciprocity

The person's country should not have a rule that prevents Indians from becoming citizens of that country through **naturalisation**.

→ The person's country should also allow Indians to become citizens through naturalisation.

#### (b) Renunciation of previous citizenship

If the applicant is already a citizen of another country, they must promise to **give up that citizenship if India accepts their application**.

→ The person must be prepared to renounce the previous citizenship.

#### (c) Residence/service for 12 months

The person must have either **resided in India**, or **been in the service of a Government in India**, or partly done both, throughout the **12 months immediately preceding the application**.

→ The person must have lived in India or served the Government of India for the required 12 months immediately before applying.

#### (d) 11 years in the previous 14 years

During the **14 years immediately preceding those final 12 months**, the person must have resided in India, been in Government service, or partly done both, for periods amounting to **at least 11 years**.

→ **Previous 14 years → at least 11 years.**

#### Special provision under Citizenship (Amendment) Act, 2019

For eligible persons belonging to the specified six communities from Afghanistan, Bangladesh and Pakistan, the required aggregate period was reduced from **11 years to 5 years**.

#### Six communities:

* Hindu
* Sikh
* Buddhist
* Jain
* Parsi
* Christian

#### Three countries:

* Afghanistan
* Bangladesh
* Pakistan

#### (e) Good character

The applicant must be of **good character**.

→ The person must satisfy the required good-character condition.

#### (f) Knowledge of language

The applicant must have an **adequate knowledge of a language specified in the Eighth Schedule to the Constitution**.

→ The person must know at least one Eighth Schedule language.

#### (g) Intention to reside in India

The person must intend to:

* reside in India, OR
* enter into or continue in service under a Government in India, OR
* work under an international organisation of which India is a member, OR
* work under a society, company or body of persons established in India.

→ The person must intend to maintain a continuing connection with India.

#### Waiver of conditions

The Government of India may **waive all or any of the above conditions** for a person who has rendered distinguished service to:

* science
* philosophy
* art
* literature
* world peace
* human progress

→ The Government can relax the naturalisation conditions for a person who has made an exceptional contribution in these fields.

#### Oath of Allegiance

Every naturalised citizen must take an **oath of allegiance to the Constitution of India**.

---

## 5. BY INCORPORATION OF TERRITORY

If any **foreign territory becomes a part of India**, the Government of India specifies the persons who among the people of the territory shall be the citizens of India.

→ The Government decides which people of the newly acquired territory will become Indian citizens.

Such persons become citizens of India from the **notified date**.

---

## SPECIAL PROVISIONS — ASSAM ACCORD

#### Before 1 January 1966

Persons of Indian origin who came to Assam from Bangladesh **before January 1, 1966** and had been ordinarily resident in Assam since their entry were deemed to be citizens of India from **January 1, 1966**.

→ **Before 1 Jan 1966 → citizen from 1 Jan 1966.**

#### 1 January 1966 to before 25 March 1971

Persons of Indian origin who came to Assam from Bangladesh **on or after January 1, 1966 but before March 25, 1971**, and were detected as foreigners, had to register themselves.

They were deemed to be citizens after **10 years from the date of detection**.

During this 10-year period, they had the same rights and obligations as Indian citizens **except the right to vote**.

---

## SPECIAL PROVISIONS — CITIZENSHIP (AMENDMENT) ACT, 2019

The Citizenship (Amendment) Act, 2019 provided special provisions for persons belonging to:

**Hindu, Sikh, Buddhist, Jain, Parsi and Christian communities**

from:

**Afghanistan, Bangladesh and Pakistan**

who entered India **on or before December 31, 2014**, subject to the specified conditions.

The Central Government may grant **registration or naturalisation** to eligible persons under the special provisions.

A person granted citizenship under these provisions is **deemed to be an Indian citizen from the date of his/her entry into India**.

Pending proceedings relating to illegal migration or citizenship **abate on the conferment of citizenship**.

These provisions do not apply to the specified **Sixth Schedule tribal areas of Assam, Meghalaya, Mizoram and Tripura** and areas covered by the **Inner Line**.

---

## LOSS OF CITIZENSHIP

The Citizenship Act, 1955 prescribes three ways of losing citizenship:

1. **Renunciation**
2. **Termination**
3. **Deprivation**

#### Memory: **R-T-D**

---

## 1. BY RENUNCIATION

Any citizen of India who is an **adult and legally capable** can make a declaration renouncing his/her Indian citizenship.

Once the declaration is **registered**, the person stops being an Indian citizen.

If India is involved in a **war**, the Central Government will not register such a renunciation declaration during that period.

If a parent renounces Indian citizenship, their **minor child also loses Indian citizenship** under this provision.

However, when such a child attains the age of **18**, he/she may, **within one year**, resume Indian citizenship.

→ **18 years → within 1 year → may resume citizenship.**

---

## 2. BY TERMINATION

If an Indian citizen **voluntarily acquires citizenship of another country**, Indian citizenship automatically ends.

→ **Voluntary foreign citizenship → automatic termination of Indian citizenship.**

---

## 3. BY DEPRIVATION

Deprivation means the **Central Government compulsorily takes away citizenship** from a person under the specified grounds.

#### (a) Citizenship obtained by fraud

The citizen has obtained Indian citizenship by **fraud**.

#### (b) Disloyalty to the Constitution

The citizen has shown **disloyalty to the Constitution of India**.

#### (c) Illegal dealings with the enemy during war

During a war, if a citizen **illegally trades or communicates with the enemy**, this can be a ground for deprivation.

#### (d) Imprisonment within five years

If the citizen has, **within five years after registration or naturalisation, been imprisoned in any country for two years**, this can be a ground for deprivation.

#### (e) Seven years' continuous residence outside India

If the citizen has been **ordinarily living outside India continuously for seven years**, this can be a ground for deprivation, subject to the statutory conditions and exceptions.

## OVERSEAS CITIZENSHIP OF INDIA (OCI)

### 1. Background

September 2000 → Government of India set up the **High Level Committee on Indian Diaspora** under **L.M. Singhvi**.

January 2002 → Committee submitted its report and recommended **dual citizenship for PIOs of specified countries**.

### 2. Citizenship (Amendment) Act, 2003

Introduced OCI for PIOs of **16 specified countries**, excluding **Pakistan and Bangladesh**.

It also removed the provisions relating to **Commonwealth Citizenship** from the Citizenship Act.

### 3. Citizenship (Amendment) Act, 2005

Expanded OCI to PIOs of **all countries except Pakistan and Bangladesh**, subject to the laws of their home countries regarding dual citizenship.

#### Important clarification

OCI is not dual citizenship because the Indian Constitution does not permit dual citizenship or dual nationality, and under Article 9, a person who voluntarily acquires foreign citizenship cannot continue as an Indian citizen; therefore, an OCI cardholder remains a foreign citizen and only receives certain specified rights and facilities, not the full rights of an Indian citizen.

---

## Citizenship (Amendment) Act, 2015

Introduced **“Overseas Citizen of India Cardholder” (OCI Cardholder)** by merging the **PIO Card Scheme and OCI Card Scheme**.

**PIO Scheme** → introduced on **19 August 2002**.

**OCI Scheme** → introduced on **2 December 2005**.

**PIO Scheme rescinded** → **9 January 2015**.

Existing PIO cardholders were deemed to be **OCI Cardholders** from **9 January 2015**.

#### Timeline

**2000** → Committee

**2002** → Report + PIO Scheme

**2003** → OCI introduced

**2005** → OCI expanded

**2015** → PIO + OCI merged

---

The Central Government may register the following persons as **OCI Cardholders**:

#### (a) Persons of Indian origin

A person who is a citizen of another country and:

* Was an Indian citizen at the commencement of the Constitution or later.
* Was eligible to become an Indian citizen at the commencement of the Constitution.
* Belonged to a territory that became part of India after 15 August 1947.
* Is a child, grandchild or great-grandchild of such a person.

#### (b) Minor child

Minor child of a person mentioned above.

#### (c) Minor child of Indian citizen(s)

Minor child whose both parents are Indian citizens, or one parent is an Indian citizen.

#### (d) Foreign-origin spouse

Foreign-origin spouse of an Indian citizen or OCI Cardholder, provided the marriage is registered and has continued for at least **2 years immediately before application**.

#### Exclusion

OCI registration is not available if the person, or their parent, grandparent or great-grandparent, is or was a citizen of **Pakistan, Bangladesh**, or another country specified by the Central Government.

---

## II. RIGHTS NOT AVAILABLE TO OCI CARDHOLDER

An OCI Cardholder does not have the following rights:

**Article 16** → Equality of opportunity in public employment.

**Article 58** → Election as President.

**Article 66** → Election as Vice-President.

**Article 124** → Appointment as Supreme Court Judge.

**Article 217** → Appointment as High Court Judge.

**Voting** → Cannot register as a voter.

**Parliament** → Cannot become a member of Lok Sabha or Rajya Sabha.

**State Legislature** → Cannot become a member of Legislative Assembly or Legislative Council.

**Public services/posts** → No general right to Union/State public services, except those specifically permitted by the Central Government.

#### Easy understanding

**OCI Cardholder = Foreign citizen + special Indian connection, but not an Indian citizen.**

---

## III. RENUNCIATION OF OCI

An OCI Cardholder may renounce OCI status by making a declaration.

After registration by the Central Government, the person ceases to be an OCI Cardholder.

The specified foreign-origin spouse and minor children may also cease to be OCI Cardholders.

---

## IV. CANCELLATION OF OCI REGISTRATION

The Central Government may cancel OCI registration on the following grounds:

**Fraud** → Obtained OCI through fraud, false representation or concealment of material facts.

**Disaffection towards the Constitution.**

**Enemy dealings during war** → Unlawfully traded or communicated with the enemy.

**Imprisonment** → Sentenced to imprisonment of **2 years or more within 5 years** of registration.

**Violation of specified laws** → Including the Citizenship Act, 1955.

**National/public interest** → Sovereignty, integrity, security, friendly relations with foreign countries or general public.

**Marriage-related grounds** → Marriage is dissolved, or the person marries another person while the existing marriage continues.
