SINGLE CITIZENSHIP
 ************
Though India has a federal system with two levels of government—the Centre and the States, the Constitution provides for only one citizenship: Indian citizenship.

The citizens in India owe allegiance only to the Union. There is no separate State citizenship. Other federal countries, such as the USA and Australia, have adopted the system of double citizenship, where a person can be a citizen of the country as well as of a particular State.

	In countries with dual citizenship, citizens owe allegiance to both the country and their State. This may create the possibility of discrimination by a State in favour of its own citizens. India avoids this problem by providing for single citizenship, so there is no separate State citizenship.

Exception 1 — Residence and Public Employment
Sentence

The **Parliament, under Article 16, can require a person to be a resident of a particular State or Union Territory to be eligible for certain government jobs or appointments in that State, Union Territory, or its local authorities.**


Accordingly, Parliament enacted the **Public Employment (Requirement as to Residence) Act, 1957**, which authorised the Government of India to **specify residence as a qualification** for appointment to certain **non-Gazetted posts** in Andhra Pradesh, Himachal Pradesh, Manipur and Tripura.

Although the Act expired in **1974**, a special provision regarding residence requirements continued to exist for **Andhra Pradesh and Telangana**.

Until **2019**, the legislature of the Previous State of **Jammu and Kashmir** was empowered to [ given authority to ]:

1. **Define who would be considered a permanent resident** of the State.
2. **Provide special rights and privileges** to such permanent residents regarding:

   * Employment under the State Government.
   * Acquisition of immovable property in the State.
   * Settlement in the State.
   * Scholarships and other State aid.

These special provisions were based on **Article 35-A of the Constitution**.

**Article 35-A** was introduced through the **Constitution (Application to Jammu and Kashmir) Order, 1954**, issued by the President under **Article 370**.

**Article 370** provided a special constitutional arrangement for the erstwhile State of Jammu and Kashmir.

In **2019**, the special constitutional arrangement was changed through the **Constitution (Application to Jammu and Kashmir) Order, 2019**, which superseded the earlier 1954 Order.
