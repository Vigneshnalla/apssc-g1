# Concept of the Constitution

## 1. Meaning of Constitution

**Constitution** → derived from the Latin word **constituere**

**Constituere** = **to establish / to set up**

A Constitution is a body of fundamental principles and rules according to which a state is constituted and governed.

It determines:

* Structure of government
* Distribution of powers
* Limits on government power
* Rights of citizens
* Goals and values of the state

---

## 2. Definitions by Thinkers

* **Gilchrist** → Constitution deals with the **organisation of government, distribution of powers and exercise of powers**.

* **Gettell** → Constitution lays down the **fundamental principles determining the form and organisation of the State**.

* **Wade and Phillips** → Constitution provides the **framework and functions of government organs**.

* **K.C. Wheare** → Constitution describes the **whole system of government** and contains rules that **establish, regulate and govern the government**.

---

## 3. Functions / Roles of the Constitution

A Constitution performs the following important functions or roles in a society:

### 1. Provides Basic Rules

It provides a set of basic rules that enables people in a diverse society to live together with minimum coordination and assurance.

### 2. Allocates Power

It specifies who has the power to make decisions in a society and decides how the government will be constituted.

### 3. Limits Government Power

It sets limitations on what a government can impose on its citizens. These limits are fundamental in the sense that the government may never trespass them.

### 4. Enables Fulfilment of Society's Aspirations

It enables the government to fulfil the aspirations of a society and creates conditions for a just society.

### 5. Expresses People's Identity

The Constitution gives people a **common political identity** and expresses their **shared values**, such as **justice, liberty, equality and fraternity**, and **aspirations**, such as creating a **just and democratic society**.


---

# 4. Qualities of a Good Constitution

### 1. Brevity

The Constitution should be precise and free from unnecessary provisions.

### 2. Clarity

The provisions should be expressed in clear and understandable language.

### 3. Definiteness

Each provision should have a definite meaning, reducing confusion and excessive interpretation.

### 4. Comprehensiveness

The Constitution should adequately cover government powers and citizens' rights and duties, reducing disputes.

### 5. Suitability

The Constitution should suit the needs, aspirations and conditions of the people, including the country's historical, socio-cultural, economic and political conditions.

### 6. Stability

The Constitution should provide political stability and should not be easily changed.

### 7. Adaptability

The Constitution should be dynamic rather than static and should be capable of adapting to changing situations and requirements.

---

# 5. Classification of Constitutions

## Evolved Constitution

It develops slowly over a long period through **customs, practices, principles and court decisions**.

**Also known as:** Cumulative Constitution

**Example:** British Constitution

## Enacted Constitution

It is deliberately created at a particular time by a **special body or authority** and is usually written in a **document or documents**.

**Also known as:** Conventional Constitution

**Examples:** American and Indian Constitutions

---

## Written Constitution

A written constitution is one in which the **provisions are incorporated (written down) in the form of a book, document or a series of documents**.

It is **formulated and adopted by a body known as the Constituent Assembly**.

**Examples:** USA, India

---

## Unwritten Constitution

An unwritten constitution is one in which **most of the provisions are not incorporated in the form of a book or a document or a series of documents**.

It is found in the form of **conventions, practices, principles, charters, statutes and judicial decisions**.

Most of its constitutional rules are **not written in one main constitutional document**. They come from **customs, practices, laws and court decisions**.

**Examples:** UK, New Zealand

---

## Rigid and Flexible Constitution

Based on the **method of amendment**, constitutions are classified as **rigid** and **flexible**. This classification was given by **Lord James Bryce**.

* **Rigid Constitution (Inelastic)** → Cannot be amended as easily as an ordinary law; it requires a **special procedure**.

  **Examples:** USA, Switzerland

* **Flexible Constitution (Elastic)** → Can be amended in the **same manner as ordinary laws**; no special procedure is required.

  **Examples:** UK, New Zealand

### India

The Indian Constitution is **neither completely rigid nor completely flexible**. It is a **synthesis of both**, as it provides for **both rigid and flexible methods of amendment**.

---

## Federal and Unitary Constitution

Based on the **nature of relationship between the national government and regional governments**, constitutions are classified into **federal and unitary**.

### Federal Constitution

A federal constitution establishes a **division of powers between the national government and regional governments**. It enables both governments to operate **independently within their respective jurisdictions**.

### Unitary Constitution

A unitary constitution provides for the **concentration of power in the hands of the national government**.

It makes regional governments operate as **subordinate agencies of the national government** and exercise only those powers **delegated to them by the national government**.

### India

The Constitution of India contains **both federal and unitary provisions**. Hence, **K.C. Wheare** described it as **quasi-federal**.

India has **both federal and unitary features**.

---

## Procedural and Prescriptive Constitution

### 1. Procedural Constitution

A procedural constitution defines the **legal and political structures of public institutions** and sets the **limits of government power** to protect democratic processes and fundamental rights.

It mainly tells us **how the government functions** and what it **can or cannot do**.

### 2. Prescriptive Constitution

A prescriptive constitution not only explains **how the government works**, but also sets out **common societal goals**, such as **social and economic justice and equality**, that public authorities should strive to achieve.

⭐ **Key Point:** Procedural → **How government works** | Prescriptive → **What goals government should achieve**

# 6. Constitution and Constitutionalism

## Constitutionalism

**Constitutionalism = Limited Government**

It means that **government power is not unlimited**.

Even though the government has powers, the Constitution **places limits on those powers** and requires the government to act according to constitutional rules.

### Very simple difference

**Constitution → tells how the government is organised and what powers it has.**

**Constitutionalism → ensures that government power is limited and exercised according to the Constitution.**

### Example: Article 14

**Article 14 provides equality before law and equal protection of laws.**

This places a **constitutional limit on government action**.

The government cannot act in a way that violates the constitutional guarantee of equality.

Therefore:

**Article 14 → Fundamental Right → Constitutional limitation on government power**

A dictatorship may have a written Constitution on paper, but the dictator may not follow its limits and may exercise power without meaningful constitutional control.

### Easy example

Constitution exists → Yes

Government power is actually limited → No

→ **Constitution without constitutionalism**
