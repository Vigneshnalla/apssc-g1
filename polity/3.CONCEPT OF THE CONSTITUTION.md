## 1. Meaning of Constitution

**Constitution** → derived from the Latin word ***constituere***

**Constituere** = **to establish / to set up**

**Constitution** = a body of fundamental principles according to which a state is **constituted or governed**.

### In the present sense

Constitution refers to a **set of principles** which specifies:

* Organisation of the government
* Working of the government
* Powers of the government
* Relationship between government and people
* Rights and duties of people

---

## 2. Constitution Is Variously Described As

* **Fundamental law of the land**
* **Supreme law of the state**
* **Basic law of the country**
* **Basic structure of the polity**
* **Grund-norm** = basic / ultimate norm

---

## 3. Definitions by Thinkers

### Gilchrist

**Constitution** = rules/laws dealing with the **organisation of government, distribution of powers and exercise of those powers**.

### Gettell

**Constitution** = fundamental principles determining the **form and organisation of the state**.

### Wade and Phillips

**Constitution** = a legally important document that provides the **framework and functions of government organs**.

## 4. Functions of the Constitution

**Elliot Bulmer** identified the purposes/functions of a Constitution.

### Think of this section as the answer to:

> **Why does a country need a Constitution?**

---

### 1. It can declare and define the boundaries of the political community.

**Simple meaning:**
It tells us **what area and people make up the state**.

---

### 2. It can declare and define the nature and authority of the political community.

**Simple meaning:**
It tells us **what kind of government the country has and where its power comes from**.

---

### 3. It can express the identity and values of a national community.

**Simple meaning:**
It shows **who we are as a nation and what values we believe in**.

---

### 4. It can declare and define the rights and duties of citizens.

**Simple meaning:**
It tells citizens **what rights they have and what duties they have**.

---

### 5. It can establish and regulate the political institutions of the community.

**Simple meaning:**
It **creates the main government bodies and tells them how to work**.

---

### 6. It can divide or share power between different layers of government or sub-state communities.

**Simple meaning:**
It tells **which level of government gets which powers**.

---

### 7. It can declare the official religious identity of the state and demarcate relationships between sacred and secular authorities.

**Simple meaning:**
It tells us **how religion and the government are related**.

---

### 8. It can commit states to particular social, economic, or developmental goals.

**Simple meaning:**
It tells the government **what social, economic and development goals it should work towards**.


## Qualities of a Good Constitution

### Brevity

The Constitution should be short and precise, without unnecessary details.
### Clarity

The Constitution should use clear and easy-to-understand language.

### Definiteness


Every provision should have a clear and definite meaning, so there is less confusion in interpretation.

### Comprehensiveness

The Constitution should clearly cover government powers and citizens' rights and duties, reducing disputes.

### Suitability

The Constitution should suit the needs and aspirations of the people. It must be suitable to the historical, socio-cultural, economic and political conditions of the nation.

### 6. Stability

The Constitution should provide political stability and should not be easily changed

### 7. Adaptability


A constitution should be dynamic and not static. It should be able to adapt itself to changing situations and requirements. 


## Classification of Constitutions

### 1. Evolved Constitution

It develops slowly over a long period through **customs, practices, principles and court decisions**.

**Also known as:** Cumulative Constitution
**Example:** British Constitution

#### Enacted Constitution

It is deliberately created at a particular time by a **special body or authority** and is usually written in a **document or documents**.

**Also known as:** Conventional Constitution
**Examples:** American and Indian Constitutions

---

### 2. Written Constitution

A written constitution is one in which the **provisions are incorporated in the form of a book, document or a series of documents**.

It is **formulated and adopted by a body known as the Constituent Assembly**.

**Examples:** USA, India

---

### 3. Unwritten Constitution

An unwritten constitution is one in which **most of the provisions are not incorporated in the form of a book or a document or a series of documents**.

It is found in the form of **conventions, practices, principles, charters, statutes and judicial decisions**.

Most of its constitutional rules are **not written in one main constitutional document**. They come from **customs, practices, laws and court decisions**.

**Examples:** UK, New Zealand

---

### 4. Rigid and Flexible Constitution

#### Rigid Constitution

It **cannot be changed as easily as an ordinary law**. A **special procedure** is required for its amendment.

**Examples:** USA, Switzerland

#### Flexible Constitution

A flexible constitution is one which can be **amended in the same manner as ordinary laws**. It does not require a special procedure for its amendment.

**Examples:** UK, New Zealand

### India

The Constitution of India is **neither rigid nor flexible, but a synthesis of both**.

It has **both rigid and flexible methods of amendment**.

---

### 5. Federal and Unitary Constitution

#### Federal Constitution

A federal constitution is one which **established a division of power between the national government and the regional governments**. It enables them to operate in their respective **jurisdictions independently**.

It **divides powers between the national and regional governments**, and each government can exercise its powers in its own area.

#### Unitary Constitution

A unitary constitution is one which establishes for the **concentration of power in the hands of the national government**.

It makes the regional governments operate as **subordinate agencies of the national government** and exercise only those powers which are **delegated to them by the national government**.

Most powers are with the **national government**. Regional governments work under it and use only the powers given to them by the national government.

### India

The Constitution of India contains **both federal provisions as well as unitary provisions**. Hence, it was described as **quasi-federal by K.C. Wheare**.

India has **both federal and unitary features**.

---

### 6. Procedural and Prescriptive Constitution

#### 1. Procedural Constitution

A procedural constitution defines the **legal and political structures of public institutions** and sets out the **legal limits of government power** to protect democratic processes and fundamental human rights.

It mainly tells us **how government institutions should work** and **limits their powers** to protect democracy and human rights.

#### 2. Prescriptive Constitution

In addition to describing how the government functions, a prescriptive constitution assumes (or attempts to impose) a **broad consensus on common societal goals** that public authorities must strive to achieve.

It not only explains **how the government works**, but also identifies **common goals that the government should work towards achieving**.


## Constitution and Constitutionalism

### 1. Constitution

A **Constitution** is the basic document/rules that tell us:

* How the state is established
* What the organs of the state are
* What powers each organ has
* How those organs will function
* Relationship between government and people
* Rights and duties of citizens

### Simple example

Think of the **Constitution as the rulebook of the country**.

It tells us:

**Who has power → What power they have → How they use that power**

---

### 2. Constitutionalism

**Constitutionalism = Limited Government**

It means that **government power is not unlimited**.

Even though the government has powers, the Constitution **places limits on those powers** and requires the government to act according to constitutional rules.

### Very simple difference

**Constitution → tells the government how it is organised and what powers it has.**

**Constitutionalism → ensures that government power is limited and exercised according to the Constitution.**

---

### 3. Article 14

**Article 14 provides equality before law and equal protection of laws.**

This places a **constitutional limit on government action**.

The government cannot act in a way that violates the constitutional guarantee of equality.

Therefore:

**Article 14 → Fundamental Right → Constitutional limitation on government power**

A dictatorship may have a written Constitution on paper, but the dictator may not follow its limits and may exercise power without meaningful constitutional control.

Easy example:
Constitution exists → Yes
Government power is actually limited → No
→ Constitution without constitutionalism.
