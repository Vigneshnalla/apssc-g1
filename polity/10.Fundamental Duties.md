# Fundamental Duties

## Background and Constitutional Development

Though the rights and duties of the citizens are correlative [ closely connected with each other ] and inseparable, the original Constitution contained only the Fundamental Rights and not the Fundamental Duties.
In other words, the framers of the Constitution did not feel it necessary to incorporate the Fundamental Duties of the citizens in the Constitution.However, they incorporated the duties of the State in the Constitution in the form of Directive Principles of State Policy.Later in 1976, the Fundamental Duties of citizens were added in the Constitution.In 2002, one more Fundamental Duty was added.

The Fundamental Duties in the Indian Constitution are inspired by the Constitution of erstwhile USSR. 	USSR = Soviet Union
Notably, none of the Constitutions of major democratic countries like USA, Canada, France, Germany, Australia and so on specifically contain a list of duties of citizens.
Japanese Constitution is, perhaps, the only democratic Constitution in the world which contains a list of duties of citizens.
The socialist countries gave equal importance to the fundamental rights and duties of their citizens.
Hence, the Constitution of erstwhile USSR declared that the citizens' exercise of their rights and freedoms was inseparable from the performance of their duties and obligations.

## Swaran Singh Committee Recommendations
In 1976, the Congress Party formed the Sardar Swaran Singh Committee to give recommendations on Fundamental Duties, because the need for such duties was felt during the Internal Emergency of 1975–1977.

- The committee suggested adding a separate chapter on Fundamental Duties to the Constitution.
- The committee emphasized that citizens should understand that along with enjoying their rights, they also have certain duties to perform.
	
The Congress Government at Centre accepted these recommendations and enacted the 42nd Constitutional Amendment Act in 1976.
This amendment added a new part, namely, Part IVA to the Constitution.
This new part consists of only one Article, that is, Article 51A which for the first time specified a code of ten Fundamental Duties of the citizens.

The ruling Congress party declared the non-inclusion [ not inlcuding ] of fundamental duties in the Constitution as a historical mistake and claimed that what the framers of the Constitution failed to do was being done through this amendment.

Though the Swaran Singh Committee suggested the incorporation of eight Fundamental Duties in the Constitution, the 42nd Constitutional Amendment Act (1976) included ten Fundamental Duties
Parliament may make laws providing penalties or punishments for citizens who fail or refuse to follow the Fundamental Duties.

If Parliament makes a law imposing punishment for not following Fundamental Duties, that law should not be challenged in court merely because it violates a Fundamental Right or conflicts with another constitutional provision.
The committee also suggested that paying taxes should be made a Fundamental Duty of citizens.

## List of Fundamental Duties — Article 51A

**Article 51A currently contains 11 Fundamental Duties.**

### (a) Constitution, National Flag and National Anthem

To abide by the Constitution and respect its ideals and institutions, the National Flag and the National Anthem.

### (b) Noble Ideals of the Freedom Struggle

To cherish [value deeply] and follow the noble ideals which inspired the national struggle for freedom.

### (c) Independence, Unity and Territorial Integrity

Citizens must protect India's independent authority, unity and territorial integrity.

### (d) Defence and National Service

To defend the country and provide national service when called upon to do so.

### (e) Harmony and Common Brotherhood

**Original sentence:**

To promote harmony [peaceful relationship] and the spirit of common brotherhood amongst all the people of India transcending [rising above] religious, linguistic and regional or sectional diversities.

### (f) Rich Cultural Heritage

Citizens should value and protect India's rich mixed cultural heritage.

### (g) Natural Environment and Living Creatures

To protect and improve the natural environment including forests, lakes, rivers and wildlife, and to have compassion for living creatures.

### (h) Scientific Temper and Humanism

Citizens should develop a scientific attitude, humanism, and a spirit of questioning, learning and improving things.

### (i) Public Property and Non-Violence

Citizens should protect public property and avoid violence.

### (j) Excellence

Citizens should try to achieve excellence in their individual and collective activities so that the country continuously progresses to higher levels of achievement.

### (k) Education of Children

To provide opportunities for education to his child or ward between the age of six and fourteen years.

## Features of Fundamental Duties

### 1. Moral and Civic Duties

Some of them are **moral duties**, while others are **civic duties**.

For instance, cherishing noble ideals of freedom struggle is a **moral precept**, and respecting the Constitution, National Flag and National Anthem is a **civic duty**.

### 2. Indian Traditions and Values

They refer to such values which have been a part of the **Indian tradition, mythology, religions and practices**.

In simple words, Fundamental Duties mainly put into written form the responsibilities and values that are considered an important part of Indian life.

**Fundamental Duties → Put important responsibilities and values of Indian life into written form in the Constitution.**

### 3. Applicable Only to Citizens

Unlike some of the Fundamental Rights which extend to all persons whether citizens or foreigners, the Fundamental Duties are **confined to citizens only** and do not extend to foreigners.

**Fundamental Rights → Some apply to citizens + foreigners**
**Fundamental Duties → Only citizens**

### 4. Non-Justiciable

Like the Directive Principles, the Fundamental Duties are also **non-justiciable**.

The Constitution does not give courts the power to directly enforce Fundamental Duties.

There is no direct legal punishment merely for violating a Fundamental Duty.

### 5. Parliament Can Enforce Them Through Law

However, Parliament can make a law to enforce a Fundamental Duty and prescribe **consequences for its violation**.

## Criticism of Fundamental Duties

### 1. Not Exhaustive

The list of duties is not exhaustive [not complete] as it does not cover other important duties like **casting vote, paying taxes, family planning** and so on.

In fact, duty to pay taxes was recommended by the **Swaran Singh Committee**, but it was not added.

---

### 2. Some Duties Are Unclear

Some Fundamental Duties are unclear and can be difficult for an ordinary person to understand.

For example, different interpretations can be given to the phrases like **'noble ideals', 'composite culture', 'scientific temper'** and so on.

---

### 3. Merely Moral Principles

Critics have called Fundamental Duties merely a set of **moral principles** because they cannot be directly enforced by courts.

**Moral precepts** → principles about how people should behave.

---

### 4. Swaran Singh Committee Suggested Penalties

Interestingly, the **Swaran Singh Committee** had suggested **penalty or punishment** for the non-performance of Fundamental Duties.
But the Constitution ultimately did not make every Fundamental Duty directly punishable.
**Critics' question:**

> If Fundamental Duties are important, **why were penalties or punishments not provided for their violation?**

---

### 5. Superfluous

Their inclusion in the Constitution was described by the critics as **superfluous [unnecessary]**.

This is because the duties included in the Constitution as fundamental would be performed by the people even though they were not incorporated in the Constitution.

## Significance of Fundamental Duties

- They serve as a reminder to the citizens that while enjoying their rights, they should also be conscious of duties they owe to their country, their society and to their fellow citizens.
They serve as a warning against the anti-national and antisocial activities like burning the National Flag, destroying public property and so on.
They serve as a source of inspiration for the citizens and promote a sense of discipline and commitment among them.
They make citizens feel that they are not just people watching the country's progress, but should actively participate in achieving national goals.
Fundamental Duties can help courts decide whether a law is constitutionally valid.
In Mohan Kumar Singhania case (1991), the Supreme Court held that Article 51A (Fundamental Duties) can be used to interpret ambiguous laws and deciding whether those laws are constitutional.
In the Ramlila Maidan Incident case (2012), the court said that Parts III, IV and IVA are closely connected with each other.
- Part III lists Fundamental Rights, Part IV contains the fundamental principles for governance, and Part IVA states the Fundamental Duties of citizens.
- When interpreting any provision from these Parts, the court should consider how that interpretation affects Fundamental Rights, Directive Principles and Fundamental Duties together.
The Fundamental Duties themselves are non-justiciable. However, Parliament can make separate laws to give legal effect to particular duties and prescribe penalties for violations of those laws.

## Need and Importance of Fundamental Duties

### Gokhale's Explanation

According to Gokhale, after independence, particularly before the Emergency in 1975, some people were not sufficiently concerned about fulfilling their basic responsibility to respect the established legal system.

**Complex words:**
- **Post-independent** → after independence
- **On the eve of** → shortly before
- **A section of the people** → a particular group of people
- **Anxiety** → concern
- **Obligations** → duties / responsibilities
- **Legal order** → established system of laws

**Original sentence:**

> The provisions of chapter on Fundamental Duties would have a sobering effect on these restless spirits who have had a host of anti-national, subversive and unconstitutional agitations in the past.

**Simplified sentence:**
He argued that the Fundamental Duties could encourage people involved in disruptive or unconstitutional activities to act more responsibly.

**Complex words:**
- **Sobering effect** → effect that makes someone more serious or responsible
- **Restless spirits** → people who are constantly involved in disruptive activities
- **A host of** → a large number of
- **Subversive** → intended to undermine or disrupt an established system
- **Agitations** → organized protests or campaigns
