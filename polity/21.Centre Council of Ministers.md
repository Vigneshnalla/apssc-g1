# CONSTITUTIONAL PROVISIONS — COUNCIL OF MINISTERS

India follows a parliamentary system based on the British model. Therefore, the **Council of Ministers headed by the Prime Minister is the real executive authority in India**.

The Constitution does not explain the parliamentary system in complete detail. However, **Articles 74 and 75** broadly describe its basic principles.

**Article 74** deals with the status of the Council of Ministers, while **Article 75** deals with the appointment, tenure, responsibility, qualification, oath, and salaries and allowances of the ministers.

---

# CONSTITUTIONAL PROVISIONS

## Article 74 — Council of Ministers to Aid and Advise President

### 1. Council of Ministers to Aid and Advise the President

There must be a **Council of Ministers headed by the Prime Minister** to aid and advise the President.

The President shall act in accordance with this advice.

However, the President may require the Council of Ministers to reconsider such advice. But after reconsideration, the President **must act in accordance with the advice given again**.

### 2. Advice of Ministers

The advice tendered by Ministers to the President **shall not be inquired into in any court**.

---

# Article 75 — Other Provisions as to Ministers

### 1. Appointment of Ministers

The Prime Minister shall be appointed by the President and the other Ministers shall be appointed by the President **on the advice of the Prime Minister**.

### 2. Maximum Strength of Council of Ministers

The total number of ministers, including the Prime Minister, in the Council of Ministers shall not exceed **15% of the total strength of the Lok Sabha**.

This provision was added by the **91st Amendment Act of 2003**.

The limit is intended to prevent an unnecessarily large Council of Ministers.

### 3. Disqualification on Ground of Defection

A member of either House of Parliament belonging to any political party who is disqualified on the ground of defection shall also be **disqualified to be appointed as a minister**.

This provision was also added by the **91st Amendment Act of 2003**.

### 4. Tenure of Ministers

The ministers shall hold office **during the pleasure of the President**.

### 5. Collective Responsibility

The Council of Ministers shall be **collectively responsible to the Lok Sabha**.

### 6. Oath of Ministers

The President shall administer the **oaths of office and secrecy** to a minister.

### 7. Minister Who Is Not a Member of Parliament

A person who is not a member of Parliament can remain a minister for a **maximum of six consecutive months**.

If the person does not become a member of either House of Parliament within six months, they **cease to be a minister**.

### 8. Salaries and Allowances

The salaries and allowances of ministers shall be determined by **Parliament**.

---

# Article 77 — Conduct of Business of the Government of India

### 1. Executive Action

All executive action of the Government of India shall be **expressed to be taken in the name of the President**.

### 2. Authentication of Orders and Instruments

Orders and other instruments made and executed in the **name of the President** must be authenticated in the manner prescribed by rules made by the President.

Once properly authenticated, their **validity cannot be challenged on the ground that they were not actually made or executed by the President**.

**Meaning:** The President's name is used for official government orders. These orders are authenticated according to prescribed rules, usually by an authorised officer.

Once the order is properly authenticated, nobody can challenge it by saying:

> “The President did not personally make or sign this order, so it is invalid.”

### 3. Conduct of Government Business

The President makes rules for the **convenient conduct of government business** and for allocating different government departments/business among ministers.

---

# Article 78 — Duties of Prime Minister

It shall be the duty of the Prime Minister:

### 1. Communication of Decisions

To communicate to the President all decisions of the Council of Ministers relating to the **administration of the affairs of the Union and proposals for legislation**.

### 2. Furnishing Information

To furnish such information relating to the **administration of the affairs of the Union and proposals for legislation** as the President may call for.

### 3. Submission of Matters for Consideration

If the President so requires, to submit for the consideration of the Council of Ministers any matter on which a decision has been taken by a Minister but which has **not been considered by the Council**.

---

# Article 88 — Rights of Ministers as Respects the Houses

Every minister has the **right to speak and participate** in the proceedings of either House of Parliament, joint sittings of both Houses, and any Parliamentary Committee of which the minister is a member.

However, the minister **cannot vote** unless they are a member of that House.

---

# NATURE OF ADVICE BY MINISTERS

Article 74 provides for a Council of Ministers with the Prime Minister at the head to aid and advise the President in the exercise of his/her functions.

The **42nd and 44th Constitutional Amendment Acts** have made the advice binding on the President.

Further, the nature of advice tendered by ministers to the President **cannot be inquired into by any court**.

This provision emphasises the **intimate and confidential relationship** between the President and the ministers.

### Constitutional Amendments

* **42nd Amendment, 1976** → made the President bound by ministerial advice.

* **44th Amendment, 1978** → gave the President the power to send the advice back **once for reconsideration**.

---

## U.N.R. Rao Case (1971)

In the **U.N.R. Rao case (1971)**, the Supreme Court held that the **Council of Ministers continues to exist even after the Lok Sabha is dissolved**.

* Article 74 is **mandatory** and, therefore, the President cannot exercise executive power without the aid and advice of the Council of Ministers.

* Any exercise of executive power without the aid and advice of the Council of Ministers will be **unconstitutional as being violative of Article 74**.

---

## Shamsher Singh Case (1974)

Again, in the **Shamsher Singh case (1974)**, the Supreme Court held that:

> “Wherever the Constitution requires the satisfaction of the President, the satisfaction is not the personal satisfaction of the President but it is the satisfaction of the Council of Ministers with whose aid and on whose advice the President exercises his/her powers and functions.”

---

# APPOINTMENT OF MINISTERS

The Prime Minister is appointed by the President, while the other ministers are appointed by the President **on the advice of the Prime Minister**.

This means that the President can appoint only those persons as ministers who are **recommended by the Prime Minister**.

Usually, members of Parliament, either from the **Lok Sabha or Rajya Sabha**, are appointed as ministers.

A person who is not a member of either House of Parliament can also be appointed as a minister. But, within six months, he/she must become a member, either by **election or nomination**, of either House of Parliament. Otherwise, he/she ceases to be a minister.

A minister who is a member of one House of Parliament has the right to **speak and take part in the proceedings of the other House also**, but he/she can vote only in the House of which he/she is a member.

---

# OATH AND SALARY OF MINISTERS

Before a minister enters upon his/her office, the President administers to him/her the **oaths of office and secrecy**.

## Oath of Office

In his/her oath of office, the minister swears:

1. To bear true faith and allegiance to the **Constitution of India**.

2. To uphold the **sovereignty and integrity of India**.

3. To faithfully and conscientiously discharge the duties of his/her office.

4. To do right to all manner of people in accordance with the **Constitution and the law**, without fear or favour, affection or ill will.

## Oath of Secrecy

In his/her oath of secrecy, the minister swears that he/she will not directly or indirectly communicate or reveal to any person(s) any matter that is brought under his/her consideration or becomes known to him/her as a Union minister, except as may be required for the due discharge of his/her duties as such minister.

## Devi Lal — Deputy Prime Minister

In 1990, **Devi Lal's oath as Deputy Prime Minister** was challenged on the ground that the Constitution provides only for the **Prime Minister and Ministers**.

The Supreme Court, however, upheld the oath as valid and held that **“Deputy Prime Minister” is only a descriptive title** and does not confer any powers of the Prime Minister.

It further held that describing a minister as **Deputy Prime Minister, Minister of State, or Deputy Minister**, even though these designations are not mentioned in the Constitution, does not invalidate the oath, provided that the **substantive part of the constitutional oath is correctly taken**.

## Salary and Allowances

The salaries and allowances of ministers are determined by **Parliament from time to time**.

A minister receives the salary and allowances payable under the law governing ministers.

Additionally, he/she gets a **sumptuary allowance** according to his/her rank, free accommodation, travelling allowance, medical facilities, etc.

In 2001, the sumptuary allowance was raised as follows:

* **Prime Minister:** ₹1,500 → **₹3,000 per month**

* **Cabinet Minister:** ₹1,000 → **₹2,000 per month**

* **Minister of State:** ₹500 → **₹1,000 per month**

* **Deputy Minister:** ₹300 → **₹600 per month**

# RESPONSIBILITY OF MINISTERS

## Collective Responsibility

The fundamental principle underlying the working of the parliamentary system of government is the principle of **collective responsibility**.

**Article 75** clearly states that the **Council of Ministers is collectively responsible to the Lok Sabha**.

This means that all the ministers own **joint responsibility** to the Lok Sabha for all their acts of **omission and commission**.

They work as a team and **swim or sink together**.

When the Lok Sabha passes a **no-confidence motion** against the Council of Ministers, all the ministers have to resign, including those ministers who are from the **Rajya Sabha**.

Alternatively, the Council of Ministers can advise the President to **dissolve the Lok Sabha and call for fresh elections**.

If the Council of Ministers has lost the confidence of the Lok Sabha, the President may **consider whether an alternative government can be formed before dissolving the Lok Sabha**.

The principle of collective responsibility also means that **Cabinet decisions bind all Cabinet ministers (and other ministers), even if they had disagreed with the decision during the Cabinet meeting**.

It is the duty of every minister to stand by Cabinet decisions and support them both **within and outside Parliament**.

If any minister disagrees with a Cabinet decision and is not prepared to defend it, **he/she must resign**.

Several ministers have resigned in the past owing to their differences with the Cabinet.

For example:

1. **Dr. B.R. Ambedkar** resigned from the Union Cabinet in **1951** due to differences with his colleagues over the **Hindu Code Bill**.

2. **C.D. Deshmukh** resigned due to his differences on the **policy of reorganisation of states**.

3. **Arif Mohammed Khan** resigned due to his opposition to the **Muslim Women (Protection of Rights on Divorce) Act, 1986**.

---

# Individual Responsibility

**Article 75** also contains the principle of **individual responsibility**.

It states that ministers **hold office during the pleasure of the President**. This means that a minister can be removed from office by the President even when the **Council of Ministers still has the support of the Lok Sabha**.

However, the President removes a minister **only on the advice of the Prime Minister**.

In case of a **difference of opinion** or dissatisfaction with the performance of a minister, the Prime Minister can ask him/her to resign or **advise the President to dismiss him/her**.

By exercising this power, the Prime Minister can ensure the **realisation of the principle of collective responsibility**.

In this context, **Dr. B.R. Ambedkar** observed:

> **“Collective responsibility can be maintained only when the Prime Minister has the authority to appoint and dismiss ministers, ensuring that the Council of Ministers works as a united team.”**

---

# No Legal Responsibility

## Britain

In Britain, every order of the King relating to a public act is **countersigned by a minister**.

If the order violates any law, the **minister who countersigned it is held responsible and can be held liable in court**.

This reflects the principle that **“the King can do no wrong”**, meaning the King himself cannot be sued or held legally responsible in court for such official acts.

## India

In India, unlike Britain, the Constitution does not provide for a system of **legal responsibility of ministers**.

An order of the President relating to a public act **does not have to be countersigned by a minister**.

Moreover, courts **cannot inquire into the nature of the advice given by ministers to the President**.

# COMPOSITION OF THE COUNCIL OF MINISTERS

The **Council of Ministers** consists of three categories of ministers:

1. **Cabinet Ministers**

2. **Ministers of State**

3. **Deputy Ministers**

The difference between them lies in their **rank, emoluments, and political importance**.

At the top of all these ministers stands the **Prime Minister**, who is the **head of the Council of Ministers and the central figure in the Union executive**.

Sometimes, a **Deputy Prime Minister** may also be appointed. Deputy Prime Ministers are appointed mostly for **political reasons**.

---

## 1. CABINET MINISTERS

Cabinet Ministers head the **important ministries** of the Central Government, such as:

* Home
* Defence
* Finance
* External Affairs

They are **members of the Cabinet**, attend its meetings, and play an important role in **deciding government policies**.

Thus, their responsibilities extend over the **entire range of the Central Government**.

**Simple meaning:**

**Cabinet Minister = senior minister + heads an important ministry + member of Cabinet + participates in major policy decisions.**

---

## 2. MINISTERS OF STATE

A **Minister of State** can either:

### A. Have independent charge

A Minister of State may be given **independent charge of a ministry or department**.

In this case:

* They perform the **same functions** and exercise the **same powers** in relation to their ministry/department as Cabinet Ministers.

* However, they are **not members of the Cabinet**.

* They normally **do not attend Cabinet meetings**, unless specially invited when matters relating to their ministry/department are being considered.

### B. Work under a Cabinet Minister

A Minister of State may be **attached to a Cabinet Minister**.

In this case, the Minister of State works under the **supervision and guidance** of the Cabinet Minister and under their **overall charge and responsibility**.

They may:

* Handle a **particular department** within that ministry; or

* Handle **specific areas of work** assigned by the Cabinet Minister.

**Simple meaning:**

**Minister of State = junior to Cabinet Minister; either independently handles a ministry or works under a Cabinet Minister.**

---

# DEPUTY MINISTERS

Next in rank are the **Deputy Ministers**.

* They are **not given independent charge** of any ministry or department.

* They are attached to **Cabinet Ministers or Ministers of State**.

* They assist them in their **administrative, political, and parliamentary duties**.

* They are **not members of the Cabinet** and do not attend **Cabinet meetings**.

---

# PARLIAMENTARY SECRETARIES

There is one more category called **Parliamentary Secretaries**.

* They assist senior ministers in parliamentary duties.

* They are **not members of the Council of Ministers**.

* They are appointed by the **Prime Minister**, not by the President.

* They have **no department under their control**.

* They are attached to **senior ministers (Cabinet Ministers)**.

---

# COUNCIL OF MINISTERS VS CABINET

The words **“Council of Ministers”** and **“Cabinet”** are often used interchangeably, though there is a definite distinction between them. They differ from each other in respect of **composition, functions, and role**.

# ROLE OF CABINET

1. It is the **highest decision-making authority** in major government decision-making.

2. It is the **chief policy-formulating body** of the Central Government.

3. It is the **supreme real executive authority** of the Central Government.

4. It is the **chief coordinator of Central administration**.

5. It is an **advisory body to the President**, and its advice is binding on him/her.

6. It is the **chief crisis manager** and thus deals with major **emergency situations**.

7. It deals with all **major legislative and financial matters**.

8. It exercises control over **higher appointments**, like constitutional authorities and senior secretariat administrators.

9. It deals with **foreign policy and foreign affairs**.

---

# ROLE DESCRIPTIONS

The various comments made by eminent political scientists and constitutional experts regarding the **role of the Cabinet in Britain** also hold good in the **Indian context**. These are mentioned below:

* **Ramsay Muir:** “The Cabinet is the steering wheel of the ship of the state.”

* **Lowell:** “The Cabinet is the keystone of the political arch.”

* **Sir John Marriott:** “The Cabinet is the pivot around which the whole political machinery revolves.”

* **Gladstone:** “The Cabinet is the solar orb around which the other bodies revolve.”

* **Barker:** “The Cabinet is the magnet of policy.”

* **Bagehot:** “The Cabinet is a hyphen that joins, the buckle that binds the executive and legislative departments together.”

* **Sir Ivor Jennings:** “The Cabinet is the core of the British Constitutional System. It provides unity to the British system of Government.”

* **L.S. Amery:** “The Cabinet is the central directing instrument of Government.”

## Dictatorship of the Cabinet

The position of the Cabinet in the British Government has become so strong that **Ramsay Muir** referred to it as the **“Dictatorship of the Cabinet”**.

In his book *How Britain is Governed*, he writes:

> “A body which wields such powers as these may fairly be described as ‘omnipotent’ in theory, however incapable it may be of using its omnipotence. Its position is dictatorship, only qualified by publicity. This dictatorship is far more absolute than it was two generations ago.”

The same description holds good in the **Indian context** too.

# KITCHEN CABINET

The **Cabinet**, a small body consisting of the **Prime Minister as its head and the Cabinet Ministers**, is the highest decision-making body in the formal sense.

However, a still smaller body called the **“Inner Cabinet” or “Kitchen Cabinet”** has become the real centre of power.

This **informal body** consists of the **Prime Minister and a few influential colleagues in whom he/she has faith** and with whom he/she can discuss every problem.

It advises the Prime Minister on important **political and administrative issues** and assists him/her in making **crucial decisions**.

It is composed of not only **Cabinet Ministers** but also **outsiders**, like friends and family members of the Prime Minister.

Every Prime Minister in India has had his/her **“Inner Cabinet” — a circle within a circle**.

During the era of **Indira Gandhi**, the “Inner Cabinet”, which came to be called the **“Kitchen Cabinet”**, was particularly powerful.

Prime Ministers have **chosen to use the “Inner Cabinet” (an extra-constitutional body)** because of its following advantages:

## Merits

1. Being a **small unit**, it is a much more **efficient decision-making body** than a large Cabinet.

2. It can **meet more often** and deal with business much more **expeditiously** than the large Cabinet.

3. It helps the Prime Minister in **maintaining secrecy** while making decisions on important political issues.

## Demerits

However, it has many demerits also:

1. It **reduces the authority and status of the Cabinet** as the highest decision-making body.

2. It can **bypass the formal governmental process** by allowing people who are not part of the government to influence government decisions.

## Kitchen Cabinet in Other Countries

The phenomenon of **“Kitchen Cabinet”**—where decisions are **cooked and placed before the Cabinet for formal approval**—is not unique to India.

It also exists in the **USA and Britain** and is quite powerful in **influencing government decisions** there.
