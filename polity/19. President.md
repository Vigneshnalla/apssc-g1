# Union Executive — President of India

Articles **52 to 78** in Part V of the Constitution deal with the **Union Executive**.

The Union Executive consists of the **President, the Vice-President, the Prime Minister, the Council of Ministers and the Attorney General of India**.

### President of India

The President is the **head of the Indian State**. He/she is the **first citizen of India** and acts as the symbol of **unity, integrity and solidarity of the nation**.

### Election of the President

The President is elected not directly by the people but by members of an **Electoral College** consisting of:

1. The elected members of both the Houses of Parliament.
2. The elected members of the Legislative Assemblies of the States.
3. The elected members of the Legislative Assemblies of the Union Territories of **Delhi and Puducherry**.

Only **elected MPs and elected MLAs** of the specified legislatures participate.

### The following do not vote in the Presidential election:

* Nominated MPs of Lok Sabha and Rajya Sabha.
* Nominated members of State Legislative Assemblies.
* Members of State Legislative Councils, whether elected or nominated (MLCs).
* Nominated members of the Puducherry Legislative Assembly.
* Delhi's Legislative Assembly has no nominated members.

### Anglo-Indian Nomination

The **104th Constitutional Amendment Act, 2019** discontinued the provision for nominating [appointing] Anglo-Indian members to the Lok Sabha and State Legislative Assemblies. This provision ended on **25 January 2020**.

### Dissolution of Legislative Assembly

If a Legislative Assembly is **dissolved**, its former members cannot vote in the Presidential election, even if a new election has not yet taken place.

### Principle of Representation

The Constitution ensures **uniformity in representation among the States** and **parity between all the States together and the Union** in the election of the President.

**Means:**

The system is designed so that:

* State representation is balanced among States

**and**

* States as a group have a balance with the Union (Parliament).

### Value of Votes

### Value of an MLA's Vote

The value of an MLA's vote is calculated by dividing the State's population by the number of elected MLAs, and then considering the number of complete multiples of 1,000 in the result.

**Value of vote of an MLA =**
(State population ÷ Total elected MLAs) × 1 / 1,000

### Value of an MP's Vote

The value of an elected MP's vote is calculated by dividing the total value of votes of all elected State MLAs by the total number of elected MPs of both Houses of Parliament.

**Value of the vote of an MP =**

Total value of all MLA votes
÷
Total elected MPs of Lok Sabha + Rajya Sabha

### Method of Election

The President is elected using **proportional representation through the single transferable vote system**, and voting is done by **secret ballot**.

This system ensures that the winning candidate gets the required majority of votes.

### Quota

A candidate must obtain a fixed minimum number of votes called the **quota** to become President.

The quota is calculated by dividing the total valid votes by the number of candidates to be elected + 1, and then adding 1 to the result.

Since there is only one President, the calculation becomes:

**Quota = Total valid votes ÷ 2 + 1**

### Ballot and Preferences

Every member of the Electoral College receives one ballot paper.

The voter must show his/her preference by writing **1, 2, 3, 4, etc.** next to the candidates' names.

This means that the voter can indicate as many preferences as there are candidates in the fray.

### Counting and Transfer of Votes

In the first phase, the **first preference votes** are counted.

In case, a candidate secures the required quota in this phase, he/she is declared elected.

Otherwise, the process of transferring votes is started.

The candidate with the fewest first-preference votes is eliminated, and the **second preferences** on that candidate's ballots are transferred to the remaining candidates.

### Suppose:

* **A = 40,000** first preferences
* **B = 35,000** first preferences
* **C = 25,000** first preferences

Nobody has reached the quota.

**C has the fewest votes, so C is eliminated.**

Now we look at the **second preference** marked on C's ballot papers.

Those votes are transferred to A and B according to those second preferences.

The process of eliminating the lowest candidate and transferring preferences continues until one candidate reaches the required quota.

### Disputes Regarding Presidential Election

The **Supreme Court** decides all disputes relating to the President's election, and its decision is final.

A Presidential election cannot be challenged merely because there was a **vacancy in the Electoral College**.

If the election of a person as President is declared void [invalid] by the Supreme Court, acts done by him/her before the date of such declaration of the Supreme Court are not invalidated and continue to remain in force.

## Reasons for Indirect Election

Some members of the Constituent Assembly felt that indirect election was **undemocratic** and suggested the idea of direct election.

The Constitution-makers chose indirect election due to the following reasons:

### 1. Consistent with the Parliamentary System

Indirect election of the President is consistent with the **parliamentary system** adopted by the Constitution.

Under the parliamentary system, the President is the **nominal executive**, while the **Council of Ministers headed by the Prime Minister exercises the real executive power**.

It would have been unusual to directly elect the President through the people while not giving the President real executive power.

### 2. Cost and Time

The direct election of the President would have been very **costly and time- and energy-consuming** due to the vast size of the electorate.

Such a large election was considered unnecessary because the President is mainly a **symbolic/constitutional head**.

### 3. Election by Parliament Alone

Some members of the Constituent Assembly suggested that the President should be elected by the members of the **two Houses of Parliament alone**.

The Constitution-makers rejected this idea because if one political party dominated Parliament, it would likely choose a President from that party.

Such a President would not adequately represent the **States as well as the Union**.

The present Electoral College gives both the **Union and the States** a role in electing the President.

## Objections to the Terms “Proportional Representation” and “Single Transferable Vote”

Some members of the Constituent Assembly argued that **“proportional representation”** is not the most suitable description because only **one President** is being elected.

According to this argument, proportional representation is generally used when **two or more seats** have to be filled.

In the case of the President, the vacancy is only one. It could better be called a **preferential or alternative vote system**.

Similarly, the expression **“single transferable vote”** was also objected to on the ground that no voter has a single vote; every voter has **plural votes**.

## Qualifications for Election as President

A person to be eligible for election as President should fulfil the following qualifications:

1. He/she should be a citizen of India.

2. He/she should have completed 35 years of age.

3. He/she should be qualified for election as a member of the Lok Sabha. The person does not have to actually be an MP.

4. He/she should not hold any office of profit under the Union government or any state government or any local authority or any other public authority. A sitting President or Vice-President of the Union, the Governor of any state and a minister of the Union or any state is not deemed to hold any office of profit and hence qualified as a presidential candidate.

### Further

A presidential candidate's nomination must have at least **50 electors as proposers and 50 electors as seconders**.

* **Proposer** → person who formally proposes a candidate.
* **Seconder** → person who supports the proposal.

Every candidate has to make a **security deposit of 15,000** in the Reserve Bank of India. If the candidate fails to obtain at least **one-sixth of the votes polled**, the security deposit can be forfeited.

Before 1997, number of proposers and seconders was ten each and the amount of security deposit was 22,500. In 1997, they were increased to discourage the non-serious candidates.

**Security deposit** → designed to discourage non-serious candidates.

## Oath or Affirmation by the President

Before entering upon his/her office, the President has to make and subscribe to an oath or affirmation. In his/her oath, the President swears:

1. to faithfully execute the office;

2. to preserve, protect and defend the Constitution and the law; and

3. to devote himself to the service and well-being of the people of India.

The **Chief Justice of India (CJI)** administers **[officially conducts]** the oath of office to the President. If the CJI is unavailable, the **senior-most available judge of the Supreme Court** administers the oath.

Anyone who temporarily acts as President or performs the President's functions must also take a similar oath or affirmation.

## Conditions of President's Office

The Constitution lays down the following conditions of the President's office:

1. He/she should not be a member of either House of Parliament or a House of the state legislature. If any such person is elected as President, he/she is deemed to have vacated his/her seat in that House on the date on which he/she enters upon his/her office as President.

2. He/she should not hold any other office of profit.

3. He/she is entitled [has a legal right to], without payment of rent, to the use of his/her official residence (the Rashtrapati Bhavan).

4. He/she is entitled to such emoluments, allowances and privileges as may be determined by Parliament.

5. His/her emoluments and allowances cannot be diminished during his/her term of office.

### Salary and Pension

In 2018, the Parliament increased the salary of the President from ₹21.50 lakh to ₹25 lakh per month. Earlier, in 2008, the pension of the retired President was increased from ₹23 lakh per annum to 50% of his/her salary per month. In addition, the former Presidents are entitled to furnished residence, phone facilities, car, medical treatment, travel facility, secretarial staff and office expenses up to ₹21,00,000 per annum.

The spouse of a deceased President is also entitled to a family pension at the rate of 50% of pension of a retired President, furnished residence, phone facility, car, medical treatment, travel facility, secretarial staff and office expenses up to ₹2,20,000 per annum.

### Privileges and Immunities

The President is entitled to a number of privileges and immunities. He/she enjoys personal immunity from legal liability for his/her official acts. During his/her term of office, he/she is immune from any criminal proceedings, even in respect of his/her personal acts. He/she cannot be arrested or imprisoned. However, after giving two months' notice, civil proceedings can be instituted against him/her during his/her term of office in respect of his/her personal acts.

During the President's term, **criminal proceedings cannot be instituted or continued** against the President, even for personal acts. He/she cannot be arrested or imprisoned.

However, after giving **two months' notice**, civil proceedings can be instituted against him/her during his/her term of office in respect of his/her personal acts.

**Civil proceedings** relating to the President's personal acts can be started during the term, but only after giving **two months' prior notice**.

## TERM, IMPEACHMENT AND VACANCY

### Term of President's Office

The President holds office for a **term of five years** from the date on which he/she enters upon his/her office.

However, he/she can **resign from his/her office at any time** by addressing [formally sending] the resignation letter to the **Vice-President**.

Further, he/she can also be **removed from the office before completion of his/her term** by the process of **impeachment**.

Even after five years are completed, the President can continue in office until the **new President takes charge**. The President is eligible for **re-election** and can be elected for **any number of terms**. In contrast, in the **USA**, a person can be elected as President **only twice**.

### Impeachment of President

The President can be removed from office by a process of **impeachment** for **'violation of the Constitution'**. However, the Constitution does not define the meaning of the phrase **"violation of the Constitution"**.

The **impeachment charges against the President can be initiated by either House of Parliament**. The charges must be **signed by at least one-fourth of the total members of the House** that frames them, and a **14-day notice** must be given to the President.

The impeachment resolution must then be passed by a **majority of two-thirds of the total membership of that House** and sent to the other House for investigation. The **President has the right to appear and to be represented** during the investigation.

If the other House **sustains the charges** and passes the impeachment resolution by a **two-thirds majority of its total membership**, the President is **removed from office from the date on which the resolution is passed**.

Thus, the **impeachment of the President is a quasi-judicial procedure conducted by Parliament**.

### Two things should be noted

1. The **nominated members of either House of Parliament** can participate in the impeachment of the President, though they do not participate in his/her election.

2. The **elected members of the Legislative Assemblies of States and the Union Territories of Delhi and Puducherry** do not participate in the impeachment of the President, though they participate in his/her election.

It must be noted here that **no President has so far been impeached**.

## Vacancy in the President's Office

A vacancy in the President's office can occur in any of the following ways:

1. **On the expiry of his/her tenure of five years.**

2. **By his/her resignation.**

3. **On his/her removal by the process of impeachment.**

4. **By his/her death.**

5. **Otherwise:** The office can also become vacant for other reasons, such as **disqualification** or the President's **election being declared invalid**.

### Expiry of the President's Term

When the vacancy is going to be caused by the **expiration of the term of the sitting President**, an election to fill the vacancy must be held **before the expiration of the term**.

In case of any delay in conducting the election of the new President for any reason, the **outgoing President continues to hold office** beyond his/her term of five years until his/her successor takes charge.

This is provided by the Constitution in order to prevent an **'interregnum'**. In this situation, the **Vice-President does not get the opportunity to act as President or to discharge the functions of the President**.

### Vacancy Due to Resignation, Removal, Death or Otherwise

If the office falls vacant by **resignation, removal, death or otherwise**, then an election to fill the vacancy should be held **within six months** from the date of the occurrence of such a vacancy.

The **newly elected President** remains in office for a **full term of five years** from the date he/she assumes charge of his/her office.

When a vacancy occurs in the office of the President due to his/her **resignation, removal, death or otherwise**, the **Vice-President acts as the President** until a new President is elected.

Further, when the **sitting President is unable to discharge his/her functions** due to **absence, illness or any other cause**, the **Vice-President discharges his/her functions** until the President resumes his/her office.

### When the Vice-President's Office Is Also Vacant

In case the **office of Vice-President is vacant**, the **Chief Justice of India** (or, if his/her office is also vacant, the **senior-most available judge of the Supreme Court**) acts as the President or discharges the functions of the President.

When any person, i.e., the **Vice-President, Chief Justice of India, or the senior-most available judge of the Supreme Court**, is acting as the President or discharging the functions of the President, he/she enjoys **all the powers and immunities of the President** and is entitled to such **emoluments, allowances and privileges** as are determined by Parliament.
