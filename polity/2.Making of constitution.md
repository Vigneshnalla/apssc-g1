## Composition of the Constituent Assembly
The Constituent Assembly was formally formed in November 1946 according to this plan. Its total strength was 389 members, of which 296 seats were allotted to British India and 93 to the princely states. The 296 British India seats consisted of 292 members from the 11 Governors' Provinces and 4 members from the 4 Chief Commissioners' Provinces, one from each. Seats were allotted to provinces and princely states roughly according to their population, with approximately one seat for every one million people.

## Constituent Assembly — Election & Representation
The seats allotted to the British provinces (a large administrative region) were divided among Muslims, Sikhs and General, with the number of seats based on their population. The representatives of these communities were indirectly elected by the members of the provincial legislative assemblies, using the proportional representation system through the single transferable vote. The representatives of the princely states, however, were nominated by their rulers.

The people did not directly elect the Constituent Assembly representatives.

Therefore, the Constituent Assembly was a partly elected and partly nominated body.

The 93 seats allotted to princely states were initially not filled because they stayed away from the Assembly.

Although the Constituent Assembly was not directly elected by all adults, it included representatives from many sections of Indian society, including Hindus, Muslims, Sikhs, Parsis, Anglo-Indians, Indian Christians, SCs, STs and women. Many important personalities of the time were members, but Mahatma Gandhi was not a member.

The Constituent Assembly held its first meeting on 9 December 1946, but the Muslim League boycotted the meeting because it was demanding a separate state of Pakistan. Therefore, only 207 members attended the first meeting. Dr. Sachchidananda Sinha, the oldest member, was chosen as the temporary Chairman, following French practice. On 11 December 1946, Dr. Rajendra Prasad was elected as the permanent President/Chairman of the Assembly. On 25 January 1947, H.C. Mukherjee was elected as the Vice-President. Later, the Assembly decided to have two Vice-Presidents, and on 16 July 1947, V.T. Krishnamachari was elected as the second Vice-President.



On 13 December 1946, Jawaharlal Nehru presented the Objectives Resolution, which laid down the basic principles that would guide the Constitution. It declared that India should be an independent, sovereign republic, that its territories should form a Union, that constituent units should have autonomy, and that government authority should ultimately come from the people.

It also promised justice, equality, freedom and safeguards for minorities and disadvantaged groups, protection of India's territorial integrity and sovereignty, and India's contribution to world peace and human welfare. The Resolution was unanimously adopted on 22 January 1947 and later became the basis of the Preamble.

The representatives of the princely states who had stayed away from the Constituent Assembly gradually joined it. On **28 April 1947**, representatives of six states were part of the Assembly: **Baroda, Bikaner, Jaipur, Patiala, Rewa and Udaipur**.

After the **Mountbatten Plan of 3 June 1947**, representatives of most of the remaining princely states also joined and took their seats in the Assembly. **Muslim League members from the areas that remained in India also joined the Constituent Assembly.**

The Indian Independence Act of 1947 made three major changes:

1. The Assembly became a **fully sovereign body**, meaning it was free to frame the Constitution without the control of the British Parliament.

2. It became both a **Constitution-making body and a legislative body**. **Two separate functions were assigned to the Assembly: the making of the Constitution for free India and the enactment of ordinary laws for the country.** These two functions were to be performed on separate days. When the Assembly met as the **Constituent body**, it was chaired by **Dr. Rajendra Prasad**. When it met as the **legislative body**, it was chaired by **G.V. Mavalankar**.

3. **The strength of the Assembly was reduced from 389 to 299** after partition, as the Muslim League members from the areas that became Pakistan withdrew from the Constituent Assembly of India. The Indian provinces were reduced from **296 to 229**, while the princely-state seats were reduced from **93 to 70**.


The areas that went to Pakistan included the whole of Sindh, Baluchistan, the North-West Frontier Province, and the eastern part of Bengal and Punjab. A separate Constituent Assembly was set up for Pakistan.

### How Did the Drafting Committee Prepare the Constitution?
The committee considered the proposals and recommendations made by the various committees.
Then it prepared the first draft of the Constitution.
The first draft was published in February 1948.
The people were given eight months to discuss it and suggest amendments.
After considering the public comments, criticisms and suggestions, the second draft was published in October 1948.
The Drafting Committee took less than six months to prepare its draft and sat for 141 days in total.


## Enactment of the Constitution
* **4 November 1948** → Dr. B.R. Ambedkar introduced the **final draft of the Constitution** in the Constituent Assembly. This stage was called the **First Reading**.

* The Assembly had a **general discussion** on the draft for **five days**, till **9 November 1948**.

* **15 November 1948 – 17 October 1949** → The **Second Reading** took place. This stage involved **clause-by-clause consideration** of the Constitution. During this stage, **7,653 amendments were proposed** and **2,473 amendments were actually discussed**.

* **14 November 1949** → The **Third Reading** began. Dr. B.R. Ambedkar moved the motion that **“the Constitution as settled by the Assembly be passed.”**

* **26 November 1949** → The **motion was passed**.

### Final Meeting and Signing

- **24 January 1950** → The Constituent Assembly met for the **last time**.
- **284 members signed the Constitution**.

### Constituent Assembly as Provisional Parliament

On **26 January 1950**, when the Constitution came into force, the Constituent Assembly continued as the **Provisional Parliament of India**.

It continued until the **first general elections of 1951–52**.

After the elections, the new Parliament came into existence in **1952**.

### Why Did India Choose 26 January 1950 to Bring the Constitution into Force?
The date 26 January already had a special meaning in India's freedom struggle. In December 1929, the Indian National Congress held its Lahore Session, presided over by Jawaharlal Nehru. At this session, the Congress declared Purna Swaraj, meaning Complete Independence, as its goal. It was then decided that 26 January 1930 would be observed as Independence Day to express India's demand for complete freedom from British rule.
Years later, when the Constitution was being finalised, the leaders wanted to preserve the historical importance of this date. Therefore, although the Constitution had been adopted on 26 November 1949, it was decided that the remaining provisions would come into force on 26 January 1950, preserving the historical importance of the date.

## Experts Committee of the Congress
	While the elections to the Constituent Assembly were still taking place, the Indian National Congress appointed an Experts Committee on 8 July 1946 to prepare material for the future Constituent Assembly. The committee was headed by Jawaharlal Nehru and included M. Asaf Ali, K.M. Munshi, N. Gopalaswami Ayyangar, K.T. Shah, D.K. Gadgil, Humayun Kabir and K. Santhanam. Later, Krishna Kripalani was added as a member and convener. The committee held two sittings, one at New Delhi from 20–22 July 1946 and another at Bombay from 15–17 August 1946. It discussed the procedure of the Constituent Assembly, the appointment of committees and a draft resolution on the objectives of the Constitution. According to constitutional expert Granville Austin, the committee played an important early role in India's Constitution-making process and prepared suggestions concerning autonomous areas, Centre-State powers, princely states and the amending power. It also prepared a resolution closely resembling the later Objectives Resolution.
	



## Criticism of the Constituent Assembly
**Critics have criticised the Constituent Assembly on various grounds.**

### 1) Not a Representative Body

* Critics argued that the Constituent Assembly was **not a representative body**.
* The Assembly was **not directly elected by the people of India**.
* Its members were not elected on the basis of **universal adult franchise**.

**Key idea:** No direct election by all adult citizens.

---

### 2) Not a Sovereign Body

* **Criticism:** Initially, the Assembly was created under the **Cabinet Mission Plan of 1946**, proposed by the British Government.
* Therefore, critics argued that it was **not initially sovereign**.
* This criticism applied **before the Indian Independence Act, 1947**.
* **Change:** After the **Indian Independence Act, 1947**, the Constituent Assembly became a **sovereign body**.

**People/authority involved:** British Government / Cabinet Mission.

---

### 3) Time-Consuming

* **Criticism:** Critics argued that the Assembly took **too long to make the Constitution**.
* They compared it with the **US Constitution**, whose framers took about **four months**.
* **Person involved:** **Naziruddin Ahmed**
* He sarcastically called the Drafting Committee the **“Drifting Committee.”**

**Meaning:** He was criticising the Drafting Committee for taking too much time.

---

### 4) Dominated by Congress

* **Criticism:** Critics argued that the Constituent Assembly was **dominated by the Indian National Congress**.
* Congress had a **dominant position among the elected members**.
* **Person involved:** **Granville Austin**
* He described the Assembly as essentially a **one-party body** because of Congress's dominance.

---

### 5) Lawyer–Politician Domination

* **Criticism:** Critics argued that the Assembly was dominated mainly by **lawyers and politicians**.
* They said that **other sections of society were not sufficiently represented**.
* According to this criticism, this contributed to the Constitution becoming **bulky and complicated in language**.

**People involved:** No specific person mentioned.

---

### 6) Hindu Dominated

* **Criticism:** Some critics argued that the Constituent Assembly was **Hindu-dominated** and did not adequately represent all communities.
* **People involved:**

  * **Lord Viscount Simon** → described it as a body of Hindus.
  * **Winston Churchill** → argued that it represented only one major community.

	The calligraphy of the Hindi version was done by Vasant Krishan Vaidya, while Nand Lal Bose decorated and illuminated it.

Originally, the Constitution did not have an officially recognised Hindi version. Later, the **58th Constitutional Amendment Act, 1987** inserted **Article 394A in Part XXII** to provide an authoritative Hindi version of the Constitution. Under Article 394A, the **President causes the Hindi translation of the Constitution and its amendments to be officially published** under his authority. The Hindi version must convey the **same meaning as the original English text**. Once it is officially published, the Hindi version is **legally treated as the authoritative Hindi text** of the Constitution.
