Directive Principles of State Policy

The Directive Principles of State Policy are enumerated [listed] in Part IV of Articles 36 to 51.

The makers of the Indian Constitution borrowed the idea of DPSPs from the Irish Constitution of 1937, which itself had taken the idea from the Spanish Constitution.

“Dr. B.R. Ambedkar described these principles as the ‘novel features of the Indian Constitution’.”

“The Directive Principles along with the Fundamental Rights contain the philosophy of the Constitution and are the soul of the Constitution.”

Granville Austin has described the Directive Principles and the Fundamental Rights as the ‘Conscience of the Constitution’.” [Conscience → an inner sense of what is right and important]

Features of DPSPs
1. Constitutional Instructions to the State

DPSPs are ideals that the State should keep in mind when making policies and laws.

“These are the constitutional instructions or recommendations to the State in legislative, executive and administrative matters.”

“According to Article 36, the term ‘State’ in Part IV has the same meaning as in Part III dealing with Fundamental Rights.”

Therefore, it includes the legislative and executive organs of the central and state governments, all local authorities and all other public authorities in the country.

---

2. Resemble the Instrument of Instructions

“The Directive Principles is similar to the ‘Instrument of Instructions’ enumerated in the Government of India Act of 1935.”

In the words of Dr. B.R. Ambedkar, ‘the Directive Principles are like the instrument of instructions issued to the Governor-General and to the Governors of the colonies of India by the British Government under the Government of India Act of 1935.’

The difference is that DPSPs are constitutional instructions for the legislature and executive.

	Instrument of Instructions → instructions given to government authorities regarding how they should exercise their powers.
Constitutional instructions → directions given by the Constitution to the government about what it should do while governing the country.

---

3. Comprehensive Economic, Social and Political Programme

“The Directive Principles constitute [make up] a very comprehensive economic, social and political programme for a modern democratic State.”

→ DPSPs aim to achieve the ideals of justice, liberty, equality and fraternity mentioned in the Preamble.

DPSPs and Welfare State

DPSPs promote a welfare state, where the government works for the social and economic well-being of people, rather than a police state focused mainly on controlling people.

Welfare State → A state that actively works for the social and economic well-being of its people.
Police State → A state where the government mainly focuses on controlling people and maintaining order.
Colonial Era → The period when India was under British rule.

→ DPSPs aim to establish economic and social equality and justice, along with political democracy.

4. Non-justiciable Nature

The Directive Principles are non-justiciable in nature, that is, they are not legally enforceable by the courts for their violation.

Courts cannot force the Central, State or local governments to implement DPSPs.

Even though DPSPs cannot be enforced by courts, Article 37 says they are fundamental to governing the country, and the State has a duty to apply them while making laws.


### 5. Help in determining constitutional validity of laws

Although DPSPs cannot themselves be enforced by courts, courts can consider them when deciding whether a law is constitutionally valid.


When a law is made to implement a **Directive Principle**, the court may consider this purpose while deciding whether the law **reasonably restricts a Fundamental Right** under Article 14 or Article 19.

If the court finds the restriction **reasonable and constitutionally valid**, the law will remain valid.

# Why were DPSPs made non-justiciable?

## 1. Insufficient financial resources

* The country did not possess sufficient financial resources to implement them.
* India's large diversity and social and economic backwardness could make immediate implementation of all DPSPs difficult.

→ The newly independent State had many urgent problems. Therefore, it needed the freedom to decide **when, where, in what order and how** to implement the Directive Principles.

→ The Constitution makers took a practical approach and deliberately did not make DPSPs enforceable by courts. They believed that aware and informed public opinion would be a stronger force for implementing them than court procedures.

# Fundamental Rights and Directive Principles

Fundamental Rights (Part III) and Directive Principles (Part IV) together aim to achieve **social justice and social transformation**.

### FRs and DPSPs — Two Wheels of a Chariot

The Supreme Court in **Minerva Mills (1980)** compared Fundamental Rights and Directive Principles to **“two wheels of a chariot”**.

→ Both are important for achieving the goals of the Constitution.

Giving **absolute priority to one over the other** would disturb the **harmony and balance of the Constitution**.

### ⭐ Basic Structure

→ The **harmony and balance between Fundamental Rights and Directive Principles** is an **essential feature of the Basic Structure** of the Constitution.

### Relationship between FRs and DPSPs

* **Fundamental Rights** → protect individual rights and are **enforceable by courts**.
* **Directive Principles** → provide goals for **social and economic welfare** and are **not enforceable by courts**.
* The State should achieve the goals of DPSPs **without destroying the protections provided by Fundamental Rights**.

### Present Position

The Constitution does not require a complete victory of one Part over the other. Instead, **Fundamental Rights and DPSPs have to be interpreted and implemented in harmony with each other**.

→ Parliament can amend Fundamental Rights to implement DPSPs, but **it cannot damage or destroy the Basic Structure of the Constitution**.

### ⭐ Easy Understanding

**Fundamental Rights → Protect individual liberty**

**DPSPs → Promote social and economic welfare**

**Both together → Social justice**

**FR + DPSP balance → Basic Structure**
